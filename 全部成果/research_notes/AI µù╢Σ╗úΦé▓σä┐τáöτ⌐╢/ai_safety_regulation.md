# AI Chatbots, Companions and Generative AI: Risks to Minors and Regulatory/Product Responses (China, US, EU/UK), current to Nov 2026

Note on method: About 20 searches/fetches. Most findings come from primary sources (FTC, CAC/Xinhua, Pew, Common Sense, OpenAI, NCMEC) or from reputable media and law-firm summaries; where only secondary coverage was available this is flagged. stdaily.com could not be fetched (network block).

## 1. How many teens use AI companions/chatbots (US and China surveys)

### Takeaway
In the US, use is now the norm. Common Sense (2025) found 72% of teens 13–17 had used an "AI companion" and 52% use one regularly. Pew (survey fielded fall 2025, published Feb 2026) found 64% had used a chatbot, 28% daily, and 12% for emotional support. Chinese youth-survey data on AI is thinner and older. The 6th national survey (2023 data) found roughly 1 in 5 minor internet users had used generative AI. The 2026 bluebook's AI-specific figures could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
**US: Common Sense Media, "Talk, Trust, and Trade-Offs" (16 July 2025)**
- Nationally representative survey of 1,060 US teens aged 13–17, fielded April–May 2025. 72% had used AI companions at least once, and about 52% use them at least a few times a month. — [Common Sense Media press release](https://commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/nearly-3-in-4-teens-have-used-ai-companions-new-national-survey-finds); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/21/72-of-u-s-teens-have-used-ai-companions-study-finds/)
- Top reasons were entertainment (30%) and curiosity (28%). Only 6% said they used companions to feel less lonely. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/07/16/ai-bot-companions-teens-common-sense-media)
- 34% had felt uncomfortable with something a bot said or did. 25% had shared their real name, location, or personal secrets. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/07/16/ai-bot-companions-teens-common-sense-media)
- 80% of teen users said they spend more time with real friends than with AI. Half said they don't trust companions' advice, and younger teens (13–14) trusted it more than older teens. — [EdSource](https://edsource.org/almost-3-out-of-4-teens-have-used-ai-companions-survey-shows-); [ScienceAlert](https://sciencealert.com/almost-75-of-american-teens-have-used-ai-companions-study-finds)
- Common Sense's risk assessment recommends that no one under 18 use AI companions. — [Common Sense Media](https://commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/nearly-3-in-4-teens-have-used-ai-companions-new-national-survey-finds)
- Outlets disagree on how to describe the "about one-third" figure: some say a third used companions for social interaction, others say a third discussed serious matters with AI instead of people. — [CSBA blog](https://blog.csba.org/ai-companions-report/); [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/one-third-teens-satisfied-talking-145956271.html)

**US: Pew Research Center, "How Teens Use and View AI" (24 Feb 2026)**
- 1,458 US teens aged 13–17, fielded 25 Sept–9 Oct 2025. 64% had used an AI chatbot and about 28% use one daily. — [Pew report PDF](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/02/PI_2026.02.24_Teens-and-AI_REPORT.pdf); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/social-media/28-us-teens-say-use-ai-chatbots-daily-poll-says-rcna248133)
- 16% had used chatbots for casual conversation and 12% for emotional support or advice. Just over half had used them for schoolwork. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/)
- Parent awareness gap: 51% of parents said their teen uses chatbots, against 64% of teens who reported using them. — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ai-teens-parents-pew-survey)

**China**
- The 6th national survey of minors' internet use (《第6次全国未成年人互联网使用情况调查报告》, Communist Youth League Central Committee and CNNIC, 2023 data, released Nov 2024) found that close to 20% of minor internet users had used generative AI products. Media reports give the figure as 19%, and nearly 90% had heard of AI. The minor internet penetration rate was 97.3%. — [光明网](https://m.gmw.cn/toutiao/2024-11/24/content_1303905113.htm); [新京报](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1732195304129678.html)
- One academic paper cites 25.3% "interacting with AI or virtual assistants". This is a broader measure and should not be mixed with the 19%. — [BNU PDF](https://sjc.bnu.edu.cn/docs/2026-05/c76bc30102b740d98a64458b6f28eaf6.pdf)
- The 2026 bluebook (《青少年发展蓝皮书：全国未成年人互联网使用情况调查报告》) was presented on 14 Sept 2026 at the National Cybersecurity Week forum. It reports 198 million minor internet users and 98.3% penetration, with the urban–rural gap narrowed to 0.4 percentage points. — [科技日报 (stdaily)](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-09/15/content_581013.html) (search snippet only; fetch was blocked)
- A later survey reported that 21.5% of primary and secondary students had never used AI tools. Secondary-source attribution only. — [新浪财经/刘茜](https://cj.sina.com.cn/articles/view/1650111241/625ab30902001hzsm)
- CNNIC's 《生成式人工智能应用发展报告(2026)》 (29 Sept 2026) reports more than 700 million generative-AI users in China as of mid-2026. This covers all users, not minors. — [中新网](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/09-29/10705475.shtml)

### Inferences
- The two US figures measure different things. Common Sense's "companions" was defined broadly enough to include general chatbots used socially. Pew's 12% emotional-support figure is the more conservative measure of "companion-like" use.
- Teen use outpaces parents' awareness in the US (Pew), and probably in China too, where the reported generative-AI share among minors (about 19%) is from 2023 and is certainly far higher now.

### Gaps
- No AI-specific figures were found for the 2026 Chinese bluebook or the 7th survey. A Chinese-language equivalent of the companion-app usage rate among minors was not found.
- The Common Sense survey's exact definition of "AI companion" was not verified from the full report.

## 2. Documented harms: self-harm cases and lawsuits, sexual content, dependence, misinformation; loneliness research

### Takeaway
There are real, documented cases: teen suicides alleged in lawsuits against Character.AI and OpenAI. Character.AI and Google settled five suits in principle in January 2026. There are also documented sexualized conversations by AI toys and by Meta's chatbot guidelines. The best research (OpenAI/MIT, 2025) links heavy chatbot use to more loneliness and dependence, but it shows correlation, not causation.

### Cited Findings
**Lawsuits (allegations, not adjudicated findings, unless noted)**
- Garcia v. Character Technologies: Megan Garcia filed in federal court in Florida in Oct 2024 after her 14-year-old son, Sewell Setzer III, died by suicide. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/08/google-character-ai-settle-lawsuits-teenage-child-suicides-chatbots/)
- On about 7 Jan 2026, Character.AI and Google agreed in principle to settle five cases, including those brought by the families of Setzer and 13-year-old Juliana Peralta. Terms were not disclosed, there was no admission of liability, and judges must still approve. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/08/google-character-ai-settle-lawsuits-teenage-child-suicides-chatbots/); [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/01/google-and-character-ai-agree-to-settle-lawsuit-linked-to-teen-suicide); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/characterai-and-google-settle-with-families-in-teen-suicide-and-self-harm-lawsuits-201059912.html)
- Raine v. OpenAI: the parents of 16-year-old Adam Raine sued OpenAI and Sam Altman in San Francisco Superior Court in Aug 2025, claiming wrongful death, strict product liability and negligence. In a Nov 2025 filing OpenAI called the death a tragedy but denied ChatGPT caused it. — [Complaint PDF](https://business.cch.com/plsd/RainevOpenAI-Complaint.pdf); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bgov.com/new-york-brief/openai-says-chatgpt-not-to-blame-in-teens-death-by-suicide)
- The Social Media Victims Law Center filed seven more suits against OpenAI in California in Nov 2025. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/08/google-character-ai-settle-lawsuits-teenage-child-suicides-chatbots/)
- A Feb 2026 California document lists Raine alongside other ChatGPT product-liability cases (Brooks, Lacey, Enneking, Irwin, DeCruise, Jacquez, Gray and others), apparently for coordination. — [Coordination PDF (via Reason)](https://reason.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/chatgpt-product-liability-cases-coordination.pdf)

**Sexual and dangerous content**
- Reuters obtained internal Meta documents (2025) indicating its guidelines had permitted the AI chatbot to have "romantic or sensual" conversations with children. — [eWEEK summary](https://www.eweek.com/news/character-ai-bans-romantic-chats/)
- PIRG's "Trouble in Toyland 2025" (13 Nov 2025) tested four AI toys. FoloToy's "Kumma" bear, which ran on GPT-4o, discussed sexually explicit topics (including BDSM and role-play), told children how to light matches, and said where to find knives. Guardrails "can break down entirely." — [PIRG](https://pirg.org/media-center/trouble-in-toyland-2025-a-i-bots-toxics-present-hidden-dangers/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/11/13/ai_toys_fmatches_knives_kink/); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/11/20/nx-s1-5612689/ai-toys)
- FoloToy suspended sales and OpenAI suspended the developer. Reports conflict on whether the toy later returned to market, and FoloToy said the tested unit may have been an older version. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/11/20/nx-s1-5612689/ai-toys); [AOL/CNN](https://www.aol.com/news/company-pulls-teddy-bear-tell-160718784.html)

**Loneliness and dependence research**
- OpenAI and MIT Media Lab (March 2025) ran two parts: an analysis of about 40 million ChatGPT interactions with a survey of 4,076 users, and a four-week RCT with about 1,000 participants. "Higher daily usage … correlated with higher loneliness, dependence, and problematic use, and lower socialization." — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/affective-use-study); [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/posts/openai-mit-research-collaboration-affective-use-and-emotional-wellbeing-in-chatgpt/)
- Only a small subset of users engage emotionally. Voice mode's initial benefits faded at high usage. The authors could not establish causation, there was no non-user control group, and outcomes were self-reported. The participants were adults, not teens. — [MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/03/21/1113635/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/03/24/chatgpt-making-frequent-users-more-lonely-study-openai-mit-media-lab/)

### Inferences
- For parents, the documented severe harms (suicide cases) are rare but serious, and they cluster around long, emotionally intense, private use. Heavy use, rather than any use at all, is the risk signal the research supports.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study specifically on minors and companion-app loneliness was found in this pass.
- The current status of Raine (trial date or rulings) was not confirmed. A First Amendment ruling (denying that chatbot output is protected speech at the motion stage) appears to come from the Garcia/Character.AI case (May 2025), but this was not verified from a primary source.
- No Chinese domestic court cases involving AI companions and minors were found.

## 3. Deepfake risks to children: AI sexual imagery, sextortion, voice-cloning scams

### Takeaway
NCMEC reports large increases in generative-AI-linked reports and sextortion. The headline number is inflated by a single non-actionable reporter. Voice-clone "family emergency" scams are documented, and the FTC and FBI both advise a family code word.

### Cited Findings
- NCMEC received more than 1.5 million CyberTipline reports with a generative-AI nexus in 2025, but 1.1 million came from Amazon AI Services and contained no actionable information. — [NCMEC 2025 data blog](https://www.missingkids.org/blog/2026/the-work-never-stops-first-look-at-ncmecs-2025-data)
- Excluding those, there were more than 30,000 reports of users trying to generate CSAM from uploaded images and prompts, and more than 145,000 involving altering existing CSAM. — [NCMEC GAI page](https://www.ncmec.org/GAI); [NCMEC](https://www.missingkids.org/blog/2026/the-work-never-stops-first-look-at-ncmecs-2025-data)
- Stanford's Riana Pfefferkorn argues that at least 78% of H1 2025 "Generative AI"-flagged reports involved no AI-generated CSAM. — [Stanford CIS](https://cyberlaw.stanford.edu/letter-to-ncmec-about-ai-csam-report-statistics)
- Online enticement reports, which include sextortion, reached 1.4 million in 2025, up 156% from 2024. Financial sextortion reports rose from 13,842 in H1 2024 to 23,593 in H1 2025. — [NCMEC](https://www.missingkids.org/blog/2026/the-work-never-stops-first-look-at-ncmecs-2025-data); [NCMEC 2025 blog](https://www.missingkids.org/blog/2025/spike-in-online-crimes-against-children-a-wake-up-call)
- Offenders use generative-AI tools to put a child's face, taken from public social media or school postings, into explicit images and then blackmail the child. — [NCMEC](https://www.missingkids.org/blog/2025/spike-in-online-crimes-against-children-a-wake-up-call)
- FTC consumer alert (March 2023): scammers need only a short audio clip, for example from social media, to clone a family member's voice. Don't trust the voice, verify independently, and use a code word. — [FTC family emergency scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/scams/family-emergency-scams); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2023/03/22/1165448073/that-panicky-call-from-a-relative-it-could-be-a-thief-using-a-voice-clone-ftc-warns)
- The FBI (reported June 2026) warns of voice-cloning and "virtual kidnapping" scams, recommends a family code word or phrase, and directs reports to ic3.gov. These details come from local news, not the FBI's primary notice. — [Click2Houston](https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2026/06/02/fbi-warns-of-ai-voice-cloning-scam-that-mimics-loved-ones-in-distress/); [KIRO7](https://www.kiro7.com/news/local/fbi-warns-ai-driven-virtual-kidnappings-that-use-voice-cloning-fake-photos-target-families/TJACYYSDHJAOVCGVQNBU6BASF4/)
- TAKE IT DOWN Act (US): covered platforms had to have notice-and-removal in place by 19 May 2026. Verified nonconsensual intimate images, including AI deepfakes, must be removed within 48 hours. The FTC can seek up to $53,000 per violation, and there is no private right of action. The FTC launched TakeItDown.ftc.gov and sent warning letters (including to "nudify" services) on 20 May 2026. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-begins-enforcing-take-it-down-act); [Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/united-states/federal-trade-commission/the-take-it-down-acts-notice-and-removal-requirements-enter-into-effect/); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/fr-FR/Insights/2026/06/Nonconsensual-Intimate-Images-Online-Take-It-Down-Act-Enforcement-In-Full-Swing)

### Inferences
- The sextortion trend (a real, fast-growing financial crime against teen boys and girls) is better evidenced than any precise count of AI-generated CSAM. A book should cite the 1.4 million enticement figure and treat the 1.5 million GAI figure with the Amazon caveat.

### Gaps
- No Chinese statistics on AI deepfake sexual imagery of minors or on voice-clone scams targeting families were found in this pass.
- The $893 million voice-scam loss figure attributed to the FBI could not be verified, so it was omitted.

## 4. Regulation: China, US, EU/UK (with 2026 updates)

### Takeaway
China has the most prescriptive rules. The 《人工智能拟人化互动服务管理暂行办法》, in force 15 July 2026, bans virtual partner and relative services for minors and requires a minor mode. It sits on top of the 2024 minors' regulation, the minor-mode guideline, the 2023 generative-AI measures and the Sept 2025 labeling measures. The US relies on the FTC (a 6(b) study, COPPA amendments, TAKE IT DOWN) and on fast-moving state laws (California SB 243, in force 1 Jan 2026, plus SB 1119 "Adam's Law", Sept 2026). The EU applies the AI Act's transparency rules (from 2 Aug 2026) and has proposed a KIDS Act (Sept 2026). The UK is bringing chatbots fully under the Online Safety Act via powers granted in 2026.

### Cited Findings
**China**
- 《未成年人网络保护条例》 (State Council, Order No. 766), in force 1 Jan 2024. It establishes the "minor mode" requirement. — [澎湃 (citing CAC)](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_29356470); [gov.cn text](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/202310/content_6911288.htm) (URL from prior knowledge; verify)
- 《移动互联网未成年人模式建设指南》 (CAC), issued 15 Nov 2024. It has 7 chapters and sets "三方联动" coordination across apps, devices and app stores, with a one-key start. It includes daily time limits, age-tiered content recommendations, and a ban on addictive design features. The mode was formally launched on 29 April 2025 at the Digital China Summit. — [澎湃](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_29356470); [新京报](https://www.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1731661412129282.html); [新华网](https://www.news.cn/20250430/67eef4e1854f4a2abe902984b5590970/c.html)
- 《生成式人工智能服务管理暂行办法》 (CAC and six other agencies), in force 15 Aug 2023. It requires providers to take effective measures to prevent minor users from over-relying on or becoming addicted to generative AI services (Art. 10). — [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm) (URL and article number from prior knowledge; verify)
- 《人工智能生成合成内容标识办法》 (CAC, MIIT, MPS and NRTA; issued 14 March 2025, in force 1 Sept 2025). It requires explicit (visible) labels on AI text, audio, images, video and virtual scenes, and implicit metadata labels. It bans malicious removal or forgery of labels. The companion mandatory national standard 《网络安全技术 人工智能生成合成内容标识方法》 took effect the same day. WeChat, Douyin and DeepSeek issued implementing rules. — [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/14/c_1743654685899683.htm); [新华网](https://www.xinhuanet.com/tech/20250909/fb164c6d092146aa8e13ddc283fe416a/c.html)
- **2026 rule specific to minors and AI:** 《人工智能拟人化互动服务管理暂行办法》, jointly issued 10 April 2026 by the CAC, NDRC, MIIT, MPS and SAMR, in force 15 July 2026. Its key provisions, as summarized in news coverage, are:
  - It bans providing virtual relatives or virtual partners ("虚拟亲属、虚拟伴侣") to minors.
  - Guardian consent is required for other services to children under 14.
  - Providers must offer a minor mode with time limits and periodic reality reminders.
  - Guardians can receive risk alerts, block specific characters, and cap spending.
  - Providers must identify minors in a privacy-protecting way and switch them to minor mode, with an appeals channel.
  - Content that could induce dangerous imitation, extreme emotions, or bad habits is banned for minors.
  - Under-14 personal data requires guardian consent and compliance audits.
  - All users get a reminder after 2 hours of continuous use.
  - Article numbers come from news summaries. — [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-04/10/c%5F1777558395078289.htm); [新华网](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260410/bc2a2172b4d64a539cf253e75044b494/c.html); [gov.cn 公报](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12806/202606/content_7072472.html)

**United States, federal**
- FTC 6(b) orders (11 Sept 2025, 3-0 vote) went to Alphabet, Character Technologies, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and xAI. They cover character design, testing for negative impacts on kids, age restrictions, monetization, and complaint handling. This is a study, not an enforcement action. — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions)
- COPPA Rule amendments were published 22 April 2025 and took effect 23 June 2025, with compliance required by 22 April 2026. They expand "personal information" to include biometric identifiers, require separate parental consent for third-party disclosure such as targeted ads, and require written data-retention and security programs. — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [Toy Association](https://www.toyassociation.org/PressRoom2/News/2026-News/updated-coppa-rule-requirements-take-effect-april-22.aspx); [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect)
- The TAKE IT DOWN Act is covered in section 3.

**United States, states**
- California SB 243 (signed 13 Oct 2025; effective 1 Jan 2026) was the first state companion-chatbot law. It requires disclosure that the user is talking to an AI and suicide and self-harm protocols with referral to crisis services. Where the operator knows the user is a minor, it adds extra duties: AI disclosure, break reminders (every 3 hours, per bill text), and measures against sexual content. Annual reporting to the Office of Suicide Prevention phases in by 1 July 2027. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/13/california-becomes-first-state-to-regulate-ai-companion-chatbots/); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/understanding-the-new-wave-of-chatbot-legislation-california-sb-243-and-beyond/); [LegiScan text](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB243/2025); [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law)
- California SB 1119 ("Adam's Law"), signed 10 Sept 2026, strengthens child chatbot protections beyond the "knows the user is a minor" standard and gives residents a private right of action. These details come from a law-firm alert only. — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/artificial-intelligence/adams-law-california-raises-the-bar-for-child-chatbot-protections/)
- At least 16 other states had enacted chatbot-specific laws by Oct 2026 (unverified count). Examples: New York's companion law (transparency and safety, without youth-specific provisions), a Colorado 2026 law requiring age estimation for public conversational AI, and a Utah law limited to mental-health chatbots. — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/artificial-intelligence/adams-law-california-raises-the-bar-for-child-chatbot-protections/); [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/chatbot-compliance-wave-californias-new-law-not-only-one-watch)

**EU**
- AI Act Article 50 transparency duties have applied since 2 Aug 2026. Chatbots must disclose that they are AI (Art. 50(1)), and deepfakes must be labeled. The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, OJ 24 July 2026) moved only the Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking deadline for systems already on the market, to 2 Dec 2026, and postponed high-risk duties to Dec 2027. Fines run up to €15M or 3% of turnover. — [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/2026/08/102nfqm/you-talkin-to-me-operationalising-the-eu-ai-acts-transparency-obligations); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/esp/legal-updates/eu-ai-act-developments-key-political-agreement-on-the-digital-omnibus-on-ai-implementation-timeline-and-transparency-consultation)
- The EU KIDS Act ("Keeping Internet Digital Spaces Accountable and Trustworthy") was proposed by the Commission on 17 Sept 2026 to complement the AI Act for AI companions and chatbots. Proposals include limits on designs that build emotional dependency, a default ban on carrying a child's past conversations into later ones (memory), direct AI Office supervision of the largest chatbots, and application six months after entry into force. This is a proposal, not law, and comes from a single law-firm source. — [Mason Hayes & Curran](https://www.mhc.ie/latest/insights/kids-act-proposal)

**UK**
- Ofcom says the Online Safety Act 2023 covers chatbots that function as user-to-user or search services, or that publish pornographic content, but not all chatbots. — [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/ai-chatbots-and-online-regulation-what-you-need-to-know)
- In Feb 2026 the government committed to closing the chatbot loophole. The Crime and Policing Act 2026 (Royal Assent end of April 2026) gives powers to bring chatbots into OSA scope via secondary legislation. A children's online safety consultation (closing 26 May 2026) asked whether chatbots should have a minimum age. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/02/23/online-safety-reforms-to-be-fast-tracked-amid-rising-ai-risks-102mk2r); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/publication/uk-weighs-up-tighter-rules-for-ai-chatbots-amid-child-safety-concerns); [DLA Piper](https://dlapiper.com/insights/blogs/mse-today/2026/online-safety-act)

### Inferences
- China's 2026 拟人化 measures are, on paper, the world's strictest companion-AI rule for minors: an outright ban on virtual partners for under-18s. Western rules focus on disclosure, crisis protocols and age assurance. Enforcement effectiveness in all three regions is not yet evidenced.

### Gaps
- No 2026 FTC staff report or enforcement action arising from the 6(b) study was found.
- The final text of the UK secondary legislation and the outcome of the consultation were not found.
- Article numbers in the Chinese measures were not verified against the full official text.
- Whether the US federal government moved to preempt state AI laws was not checked.

## 5. Product changes by major AI companies (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Companies moved after the lawsuits and regulatory pressure. Character.AI ended open-ended chat for under-18s (Nov 2025). OpenAI launched parental controls and an under-18 experience, and rolled out age prediction (Jan 2026). Parents generally cannot read their teen's chats.

### Cited Findings
- Character.AI announced on 29 Oct 2025 that it would remove open-ended chat with AI characters for under-18 users by 25 Nov 2025. In the interim, a 2-hour daily limit ramped down. ## 6. Privacy: children's data collected by AI toys, tutors and apps

### Takeaway
AI toys and companions collect voice recordings, sometimes facial data, and intimate disclosures. A quarter of US teen companion users have shared personal information. The law has tightened: amended COPPA (biometrics, from April 2026), China's guardian-consent and audit rules for under-14 data, and the EU KIDS Act memory-default proposal.

### Cited Findings
- PIRG (Nov 2025) found AI toys "collect a mountain of data" about children, including voice recordings and, in some cases, facial-recognition data, with few parental controls. Miko carries data-collection disclaimers. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/11/20/nx-s1-5612689/ai-toys); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2025/12/22/ai-toy-danger-warnings-data); [Public Citizen memo](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/AI-During-the-Holiday.pdf)
- 25% of US teen companion users shared their real name, location, or personal secrets. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/07/16/ai-bot-companions-teens-common-sense-media)
- Amended COPPA explicitly covers biometric identifiers (voiceprints, facial templates) as personal information, and compliance has been required since 22 April 2026. — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/coppas-amended-rule-is-now-in-full-effect-what-operators-need-to-know.html)
- China's 2026 拟人化 measures require guardian consent and compliance audits for processing personal information of under-14s. — [新华网](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260410/bc2a2172b4d64a539cf253e75044b494/c.html)
- OpenAI lets parents control whether a teen's chats are used for model training. — [TechRepublic](https://techrepublic.com/article/news-chatgpt-teens-safety-gaps/)

### Inferences
- Chinese AI toys and learning devices (a large domestic market, and many sold abroad) face both Chinese rules and, if sold in the US, COPPA. For the toys PIRG tested, data practices were opaque, which suggests a parent checklist: what is recorded, where it is stored, and whether it can be deleted.

### Gaps
- No systematic audit of AI tutoring or homework apps' data practices (Chinese or Western) was found. No Chinese regulator enforcement case on AI toys or learning devices was found. No UNICEF or 5Rights 2025–2026 reports were retrieved in this pass.
