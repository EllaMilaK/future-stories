# How AI Is Actually Changing Jobs and Skill Demand (evidence base for children born ~2008-2024)

Research notes compiled 2026-11-24. Method note: research was done through web search; full-text fetching of several primary pages (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, NY Fed Liberty Street Economics) was blocked by the session's network settings, so some figures come from search-result summaries of primary pages and are flagged. Items marked [VERIFY] were not confirmed against the primary document in this session. Items marked [BACKGROUND] come from well-known published studies the researcher knows but did not re-verify by search in this session.

## Q1. What do the best empirical studies (2023-2026) show about AI's effect on employment, especially early-career workers?

### Takeaway
The best 2023-2026 evidence shows no widespread job destruction from AI so far, but there is a real, growing and narrow signal: fewer young people (about 22-30) are being hired into AI-exposed, automatable white-collar jobs (software, customer service, some office roles). This is true in US payroll data (a 19% relative gap by mid-2026), OECD cross-country data and Chinese job-postings research. Researchers still disagree about how much of this is caused by AI rather than interest rates, post-pandemic over-hiring corrections or remote work.

### Cited Findings
**US payroll data (strongest causal-style signal)**
- Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, "Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of AI" (Stanford Digital Economy Lab; uses ADP payroll records covering millions of workers). Original version (Aug 2025): workers aged 22-25 in the most AI-exposed occupations saw a 13% relative employment decline compared with peers in less-exposed jobs. — [Stanford DEL publication page](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/)
- Revised version (released 12 August 2026, data through June 2026): the gap widened to about 19% (it was 15% in an earlier wave and 16% in a Feb 2026 commentary). The authors say "we still don't see any widespread job displacement due to AI, but the earlier trends we identified hold up or even grow." — [Stanford DEL news, Aug 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/); [Brynjolfsson on X](https://x.com/erikbryn/status/2087553309926416887) [figures from search summary; primary page fetch blocked]
- Mechanism (key for parents): declines are concentrated in occupations where AI *automates* tasks. Where AI *augments/complements* workers, employment is flat or rising, especially for more experienced workers. The authors report that interest rates, remote work and the tech boom-bust do not explain the pattern. — [Stanford DEL news, Aug 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)
- The Stanford team maintains a live "Canaries Dashboard." — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/project/indicators/canaries-dashboard/). A Bank of England event in June 2026 featured the paper's draft slides. — [BoE slides (PDF)](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/events/2026/b-chandar-slides.pdf)

**Counter-evidence and skeptical studies**
- New York Fed (Liberty Street Economics, May 2026), "Do Job Postings Show Early Labor-Market Effects of AI?": job-postings evidence "provides little indication of a distinct AI-driven decline in labor demand," which makes it "difficult to attribute the recent slowdown in entry-level hiring to AI alone." — [NY Fed Liberty Street, May 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/05/do-job-postings-show-early-labor-market-effects-of-ai/) [from search summary; fetch blocked]
- NY Fed recent-graduate data: recent-grad unemployment about 5.6% and underemployment 42% (Q2 2026). — reported via search summary of NY Fed series; see [NY Fed-based coverage](https://research.com/news/the-new-york-fed-tracked-graduates-for-five-years-these-majors-left-the-most-people-unemployed) [VERIFY against NY Fed "Labor Market for Recent College Graduates" page]
- Conflicting claim: one secondary source says NY Fed researchers attribute about 64% of the rise in young-graduate unemployment to remote work, not generative AI. A 2026 arXiv paper also argues that remote work better explains falling entry-level employment in the US, UK, Canada and Australia. — [secondary summary](https://www.articuler.ai/resources/learn/computer-science-unemployment-rate/) [VERIFY; secondary]; [arXiv 2026 career-choice paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15506) [preprint]
- Yale Budget Lab analyses (2025-2026) found "no clear indication" of AI effects on overall employment or unemployment. — cited secondhand in [Alex Imas Substack](https://aleximas.substack.com/p/has-ai-impacted-the-labor-market) [VERIFY; secondary]
- Humlum & Vestergaard, "Large Language Models, Small Labor Market Effects," later retitled "Still Waters, Rapid Currents" (NBER WP 33777; Danish administrative data linked to surveys of 25,000 workers and 7,000 workplaces in 11 AI-exposed occupations, late 2023 and 2024). They find precise null effects on earnings and recorded hours, ruling out effects above 2% two years after ChatGPT (the earlier version bounded it at 1%). Average time savings were only about 3%. Employers absorb AI mainly by reorganizing tasks, and some adopters move into higher-paying, AI-relevant occupations. — [NBER w33777](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777); [BFI WP](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BFI_WP_2025-56-3.pdf) (Denmark; working paper)
- A September 2026 NBER paper reportedly found no statistically significant AI-linked rise in recent-graduate unemployment. A Kharazian, Simon & Stevens paper reportedly found that firms adopting AI heavily *grew* employment by 10%, and entry-level employment by 12%, compared with firms that had not yet adopted. — [summary in Imas Substack / search aggregation](https://aleximas.substack.com/p/has-ai-impacted-the-labor-market) [VERIFY both; secondary only]

**Cross-country (OECD)**
- OECD Employment Outlook 2026 (July 2026): early-career workers in the most generative-AI-exposed occupations have seen employment decline relative to less-exposed roles, even though overall employment is still growing. AI adoption has not yet caused widespread job loss and is associated with productivity and employment growth in high-exposure occupations. Regional exposure ranges from about 16% of workers to over 70%. — [OECD Employment Outlook 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2026/07/oecd-employment-outlook-2026_a41e8b9f.html); [full report PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/07/oecd-employment-outlook-2026_a41e8b9f/7e710f54-en.pdf)
- OECD Economic Outlook (June 2026): "no signs of widespread labour displacement due to business adoption of AI technologies at the industry level." Vacancies in the most AI-exposed industries rose *faster* than elsewhere in the year to April 2026. However, unemployment among 20-30-year-olds in AI-exposed roles had already risen roughly 3 percentage points in 2025. — [Fortune, 3 June 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/03/oecd-economic-outlook-scarring-effects-recession-ai-labour-displacement/)

**China-specific evidence**
- Bloomberg (2026) analysis of about 10 million Chinese job postings, using a Peking University AI-exposure index: AI-exposed postings declined faster, by roughly 10 percentage points (one summary says a 21% drop). Several studies by Chinese university scholars link higher AI exposure to weaker hiring, mostly at entry level, and the impact was most acute for roles requiring 1-3 years of experience. A ShanghaiTech visiting scholar's review of 116 million online job ads estimated a 5.2% net loss in new hiring between 2023 and early 2026. Causation is not proven. — [Bloomberg, "AI Threatens to Make China's Jobs Slump Much Worse"](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-china-ai-jobs-slump/); [Edward Conard summary](https://www.edwardconard.com/macro-roundup/ai-threatens-to-make-chinas-jobs-slump-much-worse/) [paywalled; figures via summaries; 10-point vs 21% discrepancy unresolved]
- In June 2026 China's State Council announced an unprecedented nationwide survey to track AI's impact on jobs over the next five years. — reported in [Bloomberg 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-china-ai-jobs-slump/) [VERIFY with gov.cn]
- China youth (16-24, excluding students) unemployment under the 2024 methodology: 16.1% (Feb 2026), 16.9% (Mar), 16.3% (Apr), 15.6% (May), 14.9% (Jun), then 18.9% in August 2026, tying the record under the current method. It eased to just over 17% in Sep-Oct. Overall urban unemployment was 5.3% in August. (The all-time high was 21.3% in June 2023 under the old method, after which publication was suspended and the method changed.) — [Caixin, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-09-18/chinas-youth-unemployment-hits-record-high-102486238.html); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/china/youth-unemployment-rate); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3344145/chinas-vocational-degrees-rising-students-seek-skills-over-prestigious-universities)
- A record 12.7 million college graduates in 2026, up about 4% from 12.22 million in 2025 (Ministry of Education). — [Global Times, Nov 2025](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202511/1348697.shtml); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3333652/china-braces-record-127-million-graduates-entering-tight-job-market-2026)
- Opposite signal in Chinese job-platform data: Zhaopin (智联招聘) reports AI-agent (智能体) talent demand up 244% year on year in H1 2026, AI-industry job postings up 10.6%, and the number of recruiting firms up 24.8%. "AI engineer" had the fastest growth in postings for fresh graduates in Jan-May 2026 (+31.1%). Liepin (猎聘) reports AI-industry new postings up 21.08% in H1 2026, with algorithm engineers the scarcest role (supply-tightness index 2.64). — [Sina Finance, 20 Jul 2026](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-07-20/doc-iniimqsk8107112.shtml); [Sohu/Zhaopin report](https://www.sohu.com/a/1052498374_114984); [Zhidx on Liepin](https://zhidx.com/p/576798.html); [People's CPPCC net](http://www.rmzxw.com.cn/c/2026-07-17/3948106.shtml); [Toutiao](https://www.toutiao.com/article/7653798638946124323/)
- "De-juniorization" (去初级化): an unofficial summary of a June 2026 agent-talent report (based on Zhaopin, Liepin and Maimai spring-hiring data) says 34.39% of new postings require AI/LLM skills (up from 22.35% a year earlier), over 70% require 3+ years of experience, and postings for under 1 year of experience fell about 20%. Only about 15% of candidates meet firms' "technical + business" composite requirements. — [X post summarizing report](https://x.com/TAMPICTG87/status/2075044453979652591) [UNVERIFIED secondary; job-ad data, not hires]

**Productivity studies (why novices are both helped and threatened)**
- Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, "Generative AI at Work" (QJE 2025, vol 140(2), pp. 889-942; about 5,200 customer-support agents at one Fortune 500 software firm): AI raised issues resolved per hour by 14% on average and by 34% for novice and low-skilled workers, with minimal gains for top performers. It also improved customer sentiment and retention. — [NBER w31161](https://nber.org/papers/w31161); [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/will-generative-ai-make-you-more-productive-work-yes-only-if-youre-not-already-great-your-job) (2023 data; single firm)
- [BACKGROUND] Dell'Acqua et al. (HBS working paper 24-013, 2023; 758 BCG consultants): on tasks inside AI's capability "frontier," consultants with GPT-4 finished 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, with 40%+ higher quality. On a task outside the frontier they were 19 percentage points *less* likely to be correct. This is the "jagged frontier." — [HBS working paper](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700) [not re-verified this session]

**Layoff announcements (US)**
- Challenger, Gray & Christmas: 121,401 announced US job cuts attributed to AI in Jan-Oct 2026, about 20% of all cuts. Monthly AI-cited cuts fell from 14,029 (June) to 1,265 (October, 2.9% of cuts). "Market/economic conditions" overtook AI as the leading reason. Challenger stresses that "AI-cited" does not mean AI-caused. — [Challenger Report, October 2026 (PDF)](https://www.challengergray.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Challenger-Report-October-2026.pdf); [Challenger blog](https://www.challengergray.com/blog/challenger-report-layoffs-fall-hiring-picks-up-ai-leads-for-fifth-straight-month/)

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern across the US, OECD and China is that AI is changing *who gets hired at the bottom rung* before it changes *total headcount*. The risk for today's children is mostly about the entry-level "apprenticeship" stage, where junior work used to train people. Whether this is temporary (firms waiting to see) or structural is unknown.
- AI is both an equalizer (novices gain most when AI is used *as a tool by them*) and a substitute (firms may hire fewer novices). This suggests children need to be the person who uses AI, not the person whose tasks it does.
- In China, the AI signal sits on top of a big cyclical and structural youth-jobs problem (record graduate numbers, property slump, slowing growth). AI is one factor among several, not the main cause of high youth unemployment.

### Gaps
- No official Chinese government (NBS/MOHRSS) causal study of AI and employment yet. The State Council's five-year survey had only just begun.
- Could not access full texts of the Stanford Aug 2026 revision, the NY Fed May 2026 post, the Yale Budget Lab analyses, or the reported Sept 2026 NBER paper (domains blocked or not retrievable), so exact numbers come from summaries.
- No reliable data on AI effects on Chinese wages, or on overseas-Chinese communities specifically (e.g., Canada, Australia, Singapore).

## Q2. What do major forecasts say, and how reliable have such forecasts been?

### Takeaway
Major forecasts agree that AI will reshuffle tasks more than it will erase work. WEF projects a net *gain* of 78 million jobs by 2030, with 39% of skills changing. IMF and McKinsey "exposure/automation potential" numbers (40%, 60%, 57%) measure technical potential, not predicted job losses. Historically, headline automation forecasts (e.g., Frey & Osborne's 47%) badly overstated actual job loss, so parents should read them as direction, not destiny.

### Cited Findings
- WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025 (January 2025; survey of over 1,000 employers representing 14+ million workers): 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced by 2030, a net +78 million (7% of current employment). Churn equals 22% of today's formal jobs. Workers can expect 39% of their skills to be transformed or outdated over 2025-2030 (down from earlier editions). 63% of employers cite skill gaps as the main barrier. — [WEF press release, Jan 2025](https://www.weforum.org/press/2025/01/future-of-jobs-report-2025-78-million-new-job-opportunities-by-2030-but-urgent-upskilling-needed-to-prepare-workforces/); [WEF jobs outlook chapter](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/2-jobs-outlook/); [Chinese edition](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025-cn)
- WEF fastest-growing roles in percentage terms: big data specialists, FinTech engineers, AI/ML specialists, software developers. Largest growth in absolute numbers: farmworkers (+35 million), plus delivery drivers, construction, nursing/care and teaching. Declining roles include clerical and secretarial work, cashiers and graphic designers. — [WEF stories](https://www.weforum.org/stories/jobs-and-the-future-of-work/future-of-jobs-report-2025-jobs-of-the-future-and-the-skills-you-need-to-get-them/); [WEF press release](https://www.weforum.org/press/2025/01/future-of-jobs-report-2025-78-million-new-job-opportunities-by-2030-but-urgent-upskilling-needed-to-prepare-workforces/) [specific declining-role list partly BACKGROUND]
- [BACKGROUND] WEF 2025's fastest-rising skills: AI and big data, networks and cybersecurity, technological literacy, creative thinking, resilience/flexibility/agility, curiosity and lifelong learning. Analytical thinking remains the top core skill. — [WEF report](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/) [VERIFY exact ranking]
- IMF (Georgieva blog, 14 Jan 2024): about 40% of global employment is exposed to AI, about 60% in advanced economies, 40% in emerging markets and 26% in low-income countries. "Roughly half" of exposed jobs may benefit from AI. — [IMF blog, Jan 2024](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy-lets-make-sure-it-benefits-humanity) (older; 2024)
- McKinsey Global Institute, "Agents, robots, and us: Skill partnerships in the age of AI" (Nov 2025): current technology could in theory automate about 57% of US work hours. This is explicitly technical potential, not a job-loss forecast. More than 70% of the skills employers seek are used in both automatable and non-automatable work. A midpoint scenario gives about $2.9 trillion in annual US value by 2030, depending on workflow redesign. — [McKinsey MGI](https://www.mckinsey.com/mgi/our-research/agents-robots-and-us-skill-partnerships-in-the-age-of-ai); [Fortune, 25 Nov 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/11/25/why-ai-wont-take-your-job-partnership-agents-robots-mckinsey/)
- [BACKGROUND] ILO, "Generative AI and Jobs: A Refined Global Index of Occupational Exposure" (May 2025): about 1 in 4 jobs worldwide (25%) is in an occupation with some generative-AI exposure, but only about 3.3% are in the highest-exposure category. Clerical jobs are most exposed, and women face higher exposure in high-income countries. The ILO stresses transformation over replacement. — [ILO](https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure) [not verified by search this session; VERIFY]
- OECD Employment Outlook 2026: the lack of workers with digital skills is emerging as the main barrier to AI adoption. — [OECD Employment Outlook 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2026/07/oecd-employment-outlook-2026_a41e8b9f.html); [Outsource Accelerator summary](https://news.outsourceaccelerator.com/digital-skills-shortage/)

**Forecast reliability (historical)**
- Frey & Osborne (2013) estimated 47% of US jobs were "at risk" of computerization. The authors later clarified this measured technical automatability, not predicted losses. ITIF (2022) found that higher-risk occupations were only slightly more likely to lose jobs, and the US added about 16 million jobs after 2013. — [ITIF, Sep 2022](https://itif.org/publications/2022/09/30/oops-the-predicted-47-percent-of-job-loss-from-ai-didnt-happen/); [IndustryWeek](https://www.industryweek.com/talent/labor-employment-policy/article/21252036/nine-years-on-predicted-ai-job-loss-hasnt-happened)
- Alternative task-based methods gave far lower estimates: 9% (ZEW Mannheim / Arntz et al., OECD 2016) and 14% (OECD 2018). The Melbourne Institute critique says Frey & Osborne's labelling of occupations as fully automatable was subjective. — [Oxford Martin School](https://www.oxfordmartin.ox.ac.uk/opinion/view/404); [Melbourne Institute WP 2019/10](https://melbourneinstitute.unimelb.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/3197111/wp2019n10.pdf); [SSRN critique](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3472764)
- [BACKGROUND] In 2016 Geoffrey Hinton said "people should stop training radiologists now." Demand for US radiologists has since stayed high, with ongoing shortages. — no source retrieved this session [GAP: find citation, e.g., NYT 2025 coverage of radiologist demand]

### Inferences
- Forecast numbers fall into two types: (a) "exposure/automation potential" (IMF 40/60%, McKinsey 57%, Frey-Osborne 47%), which are ceilings on tasks, and (b) employer-survey projections (WEF), which are opinions with large uncertainty. Neither type is an observed outcome. History shows a big gap between technical potential and actual job loss, because adoption is slow, firms reorganize work, and new tasks appear.
- The roles WEF expects to grow in absolute numbers (care, construction, agriculture, delivery, teaching) are mostly physical or interpersonal. The fastest-growing in percentage terms are AI/tech specialists. That two-track pattern matters for parental advice.

### Gaps
- Could not verify ILO 2025 numbers or any 2026 WEF/IMF updates by search this session.
- No Chinese-institution macro forecast (e.g., CASS, CAICT, 人社部 new-occupation lists) was retrieved. Searches in Chinese surfaced only job-platform reports.
- No systematic back-test of WEF's earlier (2016/2018/2020) Future of Jobs predictions was found.

## Q3. Which skills and traits show durable value in the evidence?

### Takeaway
The best-supported durable assets are (1) social and coordination skills combined with cognitive skill (Deming), (2) deep domain expertise and experience, which in payroll data is protected where AI complements rather than automates, (3) the ability to use AI well, including judging where it fails (the "jagged frontier"), and (4) physical and skilled-trade or care work that WEF projects to grow in absolute terms. Evidence that generative AI specifically *raises* the return to social skills is still mostly indirect.

### Cited Findings
- Deming, "The Growing Importance of Social Skills in the Labor Market" (QJE 2017): jobs requiring high levels of social interaction grew by nearly 12 percentage points as a share of the US labor force (1980-2012). Math-intensive but low-social jobs shrank. The wage return to social skills is positive after controlling for cognitive and non-cognitive skills. Cognitive and social skills are complements, and that complementarity has grown over time. Mechanism: social skills lower coordination costs in team production. — [Deming QJE PDF](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/ddeming/files/deming_socialskills_qje.pdf); [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/nov15/growing-importance-social-skills-labor-market) (pre-generative-AI; 2017)
- Deming and coauthors' later survey (NBER w32908, 2024, "Skills and Human Capital in the Labor Market"): the economic return to social skills in the US more than doubled. — [NBER w32908](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32908/w32908.pdf)
- Stanford "Canaries" (Aug 2026): employment is flat or rising where AI augments workers, especially experienced ones. This suggests tacit knowledge and experience are complements. — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)
- A related preprint argues automation and AI may weaken the "intergenerational transmission of knowledge," meaning juniors lose the learning-by-doing tasks that build expertise. — [arXiv 2507.16078](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.16078) [preprint; theory]
- Market-pricing evidence: a 2026 arXiv paper finds market-implied AI exposure is *positive* for nonroutine interactive tasks and social skills, meaning investors expect these to benefit. — [arXiv 2606.30583](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.30583) [preprint; stock prices, not wages]
- McKinsey (Nov 2025): most skills persist but are applied differently. Over 70% of sought skills appear in both automatable and non-automatable work. Value comes from "skill partnerships" between people, agents and robots. — [McKinsey MGI](https://www.mckinsey.com/mgi/our-research/agents-robots-and-us-skill-partnerships-in-the-age-of-ai)
- Chinese job platforms: employers want "composite" (复合型) talent, combining technical and business skills. Only about 15% of candidates meet that bar (unverified). Zhaopin's 2026 AI-talent report says talent supply is shifting "from major-matching to cross-disciplinary fusion" (从专业对口走向跨界融合). — [People's CPPCC net on Zhaopin report](http://www.rmzxw.com.cn/c/2026-07-17/3948106.shtml); [X summary](https://x.com/TAMPICTG87/status/2075044453979652591) [unverified]
- Productivity: AI helps novices most (+34%) in structured tasks (Brynjolfsson et al.), but people who over-trust AI outside its competence do worse (Dell'Acqua et al.: -19 pp). Judgment about when to trust AI is therefore a skill. — [NBER w31161](https://nber.org/papers/w31161); [HBS WP](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700) [BACKGROUND]
- WEF 2025: the largest absolute job growth is in farmwork, delivery, construction, care and teaching, and robotics/autonomous systems are the largest net displacer (-5 million). — [WEF press release](https://www.weforum.org/press/2025/01/future-of-jobs-report-2025-78-million-new-job-opportunities-by-2030-but-urgent-upskilling-needed-to-prepare-workforces/)

### Inferences
- For parents: the evidence favors "T-shaped" children, with deep competence in some domain, strong social and communication skills, and fluency in directing and checking AI. It does not favor narrow test-taking skill on routine cognitive tasks, which is the most AI-exposed and automatable.
- Physical trades are less AI-exposed now, but WEF flags robotics as a major displacer. "Physical" is a hedge, not a guarantee.

### Gaps
- No rigorous post-2023 wage study found showing generative AI raising the social-skill premium directly.
- No direct evidence found on childhood traits (e.g., curiosity, grit) predicting adult outcomes *under AI*. That is a separate literature (e.g., Heckman) not covered here.
- No good data found on US skilled-trades wages and demand among Gen Z in 2026 (search not completed).

## Q4. Value of university degrees, vocational education and majors under AI (China and abroad)

### Takeaway
Degrees still pay in aggregate, but the premium is becoming more uneven by major and by the skills a graduate actually holds. Computer science, once the safest bet, now has above-average unemployment among new US graduates, though still high pay and low underemployment. In China, record graduate numbers, an employer shift toward vocational/applied graduates, and the decline of the master's-degree advantage suggest that "more credentials" is no longer a reliable strategy by itself.

### Cited Findings
- NY Fed "Labor Market for Recent College Graduates" (Feb 2026 release, using 2024 ACS data): recent computer science graduates had 7.0% unemployment and computer engineering 7.8%, versus 4.2% for all recent graduates in that table. Both majors had underemployment below 20%, versus 42% across all majors. Early-career median pay was $87,000 (CS) and $90,000 (CE). — [research.com summary of NY Fed data](https://research.com/news/the-new-york-fed-tracked-graduates-for-five-years-these-majors-left-the-most-people-unemployed); [Forbes 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/colleenbatchelder/article/college-majors-with-low-unemployement-rates/) [VERIFY on newyorkfed.org; other sources cite 6.1% for CS]
- China: employer demand share for vocational graduates rose from 8.5% (2024) to 11% (2025), while demand for master's graduates fell from 20.3% to 17.4% (official data cited by Sixth Tone). — [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1018143)
- China's first cohort of vocational *undergraduate* (职业本科) graduates had an 87.1% average employment rate, about 4.5 points above the national undergraduate average (government data via SCMP). — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3344145/chinas-vocational-degrees-rising-students-seek-skills-over-prestigious-universities) [VERIFY original MOE release; Chinese "employment rates" are self-reported by schools and widely criticized as inflated]
- China vocational white paper (Oct 2026): in 2025 there were about 5.82 million higher-vocational graduates and 5.22 million secondary-vocational graduates, 10,792 vocational schools and over 30 million students. The system supplies "nearly 10 million skilled workers" a year. — [Global Times, Oct 2026](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202610/1372427.shtml)
- China AI education capacity: 700+ universities offer AI undergraduate programs and 900+ higher-vocational colleges have registered AI-technology-application programs. Alibaba's 2027 intern intake is over 80% AI roles, with seven new AI-agent campus-recruiting job types. — [X summary of June 2026 agent-talent report](https://x.com/TAMPICTG87/status/2075044453979652591) [UNVERIFIED]

## Q5. Latest 2026 developments (agentic AI, hiring changes) through November 2026

### Takeaway
2026 was billed in China as the "breakout year for AI agents" (智能体爆发元年). Demand for AI and agent talent exploded, while requirements shifted toward experience and AI skills. In the US, AI-cited layoffs peaked mid-year and then fell sharply. Through mid/late 2026, official and academic sources agree on a hiring-side squeeze for juniors in exposed roles, not mass displacement.

### Inferences
- The 2026 picture splits in two. Booming demand for a thin layer of AI builders and "AI + business" hybrids sits alongside fewer generic junior white-collar openings. This creates a barbell that parents can't fix by pushing children toward one "hot" credential.
- For children born 2008-2024, who enter the labor market around 2030-2046, today's specific hot jobs (prompt engineer, agent engineer) are very likely to change. Durable bets are adaptable foundations (domain depth, social skill, AI fluency, learning ability), not specific titles.
