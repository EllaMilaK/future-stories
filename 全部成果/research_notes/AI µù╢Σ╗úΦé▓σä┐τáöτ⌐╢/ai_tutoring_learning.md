## 1. What do RCTs and quasi-experiments show? Effect sizes, conditions, guardrails

### Takeaway
The causal evidence splits cleanly by design. **Unrestricted, answer-giving chatbots used for practice raise homework/practice performance but lower learning measured without AI** (Turkey RCT: -17% on exams; China observational panel: about -20% on monthly exams). **AI that is pedagogically constrained (hints, not answers) and embedded in structured teaching, with a teacher or tutor in the loop, produces modest-to-large gains** (Nigeria +0.23 SD English in 6 weeks; Harvard physics d≈0.63+; Tutor CoPilot +4 pp mastery). Simply bolting a chatbot onto existing practice software adds little (Khanmigo Tennessee 0.06–0.08 SD, similar to non-AI Khan practice; rural China AI-personalized practice no better than non-AI practice).

### Cited Findings

**Harms / "crutch" evidence**
- [FIELD RCT, peer-reviewed] Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakcı & Mariman, "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics," *PNAS* 122(26), 2025. Nearly 1,000 high-school math students in Turkey (one school; ran around Fall 2023, when chatbots were novel). Two GPT-4 arms: "GPT Base" (ChatGPT-like) and "GPT Tutor" (teacher-designed prompts giving hints, not answers). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html); [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4895486)
  - During AI-assisted practice: GPT Base +48%, GPT Tutor +127% vs control. — [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education)
  - On the subsequent unassisted exam: GPT Base **-17%** vs control; GPT Tutor ≈ control (harm "largely mitigated," but no gain). — [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/unrestricted-generative-ai-harms-high-school-math-learning-by-acting-as-a-crutch/)
  - Mechanism: most GPT Base students simply asked for the answer. GPT Tutor students were overconfident: they believed they did better than they did. — [KQED MindShift](https://kqed.org/mindshift/64575/kids-who-use-chatgpt-as-a-study-assistant-do-worse-on-tests)
- [FIELD, observational panel with quasi-experimental methods; CEPR Discussion Paper, not yet peer-reviewed] Strömberg, Lei & Wu, "The Generative AI Learning Penalty: Evidence from Chinese Secondary Education," CEPR DP 21577 (2026). 30 months of panel data on 26,811 students in grades 7–12 in a county in central China. AI adoption **raises homework scores by 18% and cuts completion time by 30%, but lowers monthly exam scores by 20% within six months.** Reported penalties on high-stakes exams: about 24% of the baseline mean for the high-school entrance exam (中考) and 18% for the college entrance exam (高考). Students chose their own tools, so this is not randomized. — [RePEc CEPR DP 21577](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/21577.html); [VoxDev column "Better homework, worse exams," 15 Sep 2026](https://voxdev.org/topic/education/generative-ai-learning-penalty-secondary-school); [Education Next](https://www.educationnext.org/the-penalty-students-pay-for-using-ai/)
- [FIELD observational + LAB experiments; PREPRINT] Lehmann, Cornelius & Sting, "AI Meets the Classroom: When Do Large Language Models Harm Learning?" (arXiv 2409.09047). University programming courses: exogenous variation in LLM availability gives a significant long-term decline in learning when use is substitutive. Lab replications: using the LLM for **explanations helped**; using it to **solve the practice problems hurt**. Students with stronger prior knowledge benefited more; weaker students learned less. LLM access inflated perceived learning above actual learning. — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09047v2); [Warwick project summary](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/cross_fac/eduport/edufund/projects/yang/projects/ai-meets-the-classroom-when-do-large-language-models-harm-learning/)
- [LAB, PREPRINT, n=54] Kosmyna et al. (MIT Media Lab), "Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt…" (arXiv 2506.08872, June 2025). Essay writing in three groups (LLM, search engine, brain-only), with EEG. Brain-only showed the strongest, most distributed connectivity and LLM users the weakest. LLM users reported the lowest ownership of their essays and had trouble quoting their own work. In the 4th session (n=18, groups swapped), people who wrote unaided first and then used AI kept higher brain engagement than those who started with AI. Small sample, not peer-reviewed, and it does not show "brain damage." — [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/your-brain-on-chatgpt/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872v1); [IBM Think](https://www.ibm.com/think/news/when-ai-thinks-brain-gets-quieter); [ThePrint caveats](https://theprint.in/feature/the-price-we-pay-for-using-chatgpt-to-write-what-an-mit-study-found/3089016/)
- [SURVEY, adults, CHI 2025] Lee et al. (Microsoft Research/CMU), 319 knowledge workers: higher confidence in GenAI was associated with less critical thinking, and higher self-confidence with more. Critical effort shifted from doing the work to verifying and integrating outputs. Correlational only. — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1136647); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/02/11/microsoft_study_ai_critical_thinking/)

**Benefits / structured-use evidence**
- [FIELD RCT; World Bank Policy Research WP 11125, May 2025; not journal-peer-reviewed] De Simone et al., "From Chalkboards to Chatbots," Edo State, Nigeria. Six-week after-school program for first-year senior secondary students (about age 15). Students used Microsoft Copilot (GPT-4) for English, with **teachers guiding sessions and structured prompts**. English effect is **0.23 SD**: ITT on third-term exam reported as 0.21 SD in one version, and VoxDev cites about 0.24. A composite of English, AI knowledge and digital skills is about 0.31 SD, which is the source of the widely quoted "0.3 SD." Average exposure was only about 13 hours. Attendance was hurt by flooding, teacher strikes and work. Estimated benefit-cost ratio is 161–260. Cite 0.23 SD for English and treat "0.3" as the composite. — [World Bank OKR](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/15e1ff08-15ae-4f7a-b2a8-d146e6c113ee/full); [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/education/From-chalkboards-to-chatbots-Transforming-learning-in-Nigeria); [VoxDev](https://www.voxdev.org/topic/education/how-ai-tutors-improved-learning-nigeria); [IZA DP 19025](https://docs.iza.org/dp19025.pdf)
- [FIELD RCT within a course, short-term; peer-reviewed] Kestin, Miller et al., *Scientific Reports* 15:17458 (June 2025). N=194 Harvard intro-physics undergraduates, crossover design. A custom GPT-4 tutor was engineered with pedagogical best practices: scaffolding, one step at a time, no full answers, and pre-written correct solutions to limit errors. It was compared with an active-learning class. Linear-regression effect size d=0.63, which the authors call an underestimate due to ceiling effects. Median learning gains were more than double. AI students spent less time (about 49 vs 60 min) and reported more engagement and motivation. Limits: two topics, one elite institution, no long-term retention measure, and the AI was used *before* class as preparation, not as a homework answer machine. — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/); [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2024/09/professor-tailored-ai-tutor-to-physics-course-engagement-doubled/); [Hechinger](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-ai-tutor-harvard-physics/)
- [FIELD RCT, human-AI; arXiv PREPRINT, J-PAL-listed] Wang, Ribeiro, Robinson, Loeb & Demszky (Stanford), "Tutor CoPilot." The AI coached *human tutors* in real time; it did not tutor students directly. Students were K-12 math learners from under-served U.S. communities. Sample figures differ by version: 900 tutors/1,800 students (arXiv) vs 783 tutors/1,013 students (J-PAL). ITT effect: students **4 pp more likely to master topics** (p<0.01), and **+9 pp for students of lower-rated tutors**. Cost about $20 per tutor per year. Outcomes were lesson exit tickets, not end-of-year state tests. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017); [J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/human-ai-cooperation-improve-tutoring-united-states); [Stanford NSSA](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/tutor-copilot-human-ai-approach-scaling-real-time-expertise)
- [FIELD RCT, company-published] Google/Fab AI pre-registered RCT in Sierra Leone: 1,763 junior-secondary students (grades 7–8), 8 weeks. Using Gemini Guided Learning for at least 12 hours "significantly improved" learning (magnitude not in snippet). In a separate LearnLM result, students who got short LearnLM tutoring sessions were 5.5 pp more likely to solve novel problems on later topics than students with human tutors alone. Not independently replicated. — [Google LearnLM page](https://cloud.google.com/solutions/learnlm?hl=nl); [LearnLM tech report arXiv 2412.16429](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16429v3)

**Null / small effects when a chatbot is just added**
- [FIELD RCT; NBER WP 35620, 2026] Oreopoulos & Low, "One Click Away: AI Tutoring with Khanmigo in a Two-Year School Experiment." 18 Tennessee middle schools, remedial math. Gain of about **0.06–0.08 SD per school year**, similar to Khan Academy practice *without* AI. Students messaged Khanmigo on only about one-third of practice days and in 17% of error sessions. KQED headline: students "didn't get answers from Khanmigo, they didn't want its questions either." Khan Academy says the 2023 version tested is outdated and the comparison was against other edtech. — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620); [EdWorkingPapers PDF](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf); [FutureEd](https://www.future-ed.org/students-tried-khanmigos-ai-tutor-most-didnt-want-its-help/); [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/mindshift/66662/students-didnt-get-answers-from-khanmigo-they-didnt-want-its-questions-either); [Khan Academy response](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=22104)
- [FIELD RCT, China; CESifo WP 12837] Ma, Feng, Fairlie, Liu, Loyalka, Rozelle & Zhang, "Putting AI to the Test: Evidence from a Large-Scale RCT in Rural China." 8,647 students in grades 4–6, 315 classes, 105 primary schools in low-income Sichuan counties, run in 2021. Arms: AI-personalized computer-assisted learning vs non-personalized CAL vs pure control. The AI arm was no better: reported −0.04 SD vs both, ruling out all but trivial gains. Usage was low and unequal. This is pre-LLM "AI personalization," not a chatbot. The −0.04 figure comes from a secondary essay, so verify it. — [RePEc/CESifo](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ces/ceswps/_12837.html); [Stanford FSI](https://fsi.stanford.edu/node/39942)
- [RCT, China, university; PREPRINT arXiv 2607.10101] Randomized experiment with 318 university students: proactive, critical engagement with AI predicted better outcomes, and AI assistance produced larger exam gains for advantaged students, **widening gaps**. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.10101.md)
- [RCT, China, grade-10 physics; details unverified] Two randomized experiments gave LLM-generated feedback on regular homework over five weeks (n=83 and n=266). Effect sizes not obtained. — [arXiv 2607.08849](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08849)

**Meta-analyses and syntheses (use with caution)**
- Wang & Fan (2025), *Humanities & Social Sciences Communications* 12, meta-analysis of 51 experimental or quasi-experimental studies (Nov 2022–Feb 2025): ChatGPT effect on learning performance **g=0.867**, learning perception g=0.456, higher-order thinking g=0.457. Moderators: course type, learning model (problem-based learning largest), duration. Caveat: most included studies measure performance *with* access or on immediate tasks, and many are small. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-04787-y.html); Doo & Park, *IRRODL*, g=0.573 across 22 studies — [IRRODL](https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/8775/6374)
- OECD *Digital Education Outlook 2026* (Jan 2026): students with general-purpose GenAI produce better outputs, but "this advantage disappears – and sometimes reverses – in exams when access is removed." Tools designed or used with intentional pedagogical purpose tend to show sustained learning improvements. It warns of "metacognitive laziness." TALIS 2024: 37% of lower-secondary teachers use AI, and 72% think AI can harm academic integrity. — [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026_062a7394-en.html); [EU Digital Skills summary](https://digital-skills-jobs.europa.eu/en/latest/news/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026-how-generative-ai-can-support-learning-when-used)

### Inferences
- The decisive variable is not "AI or no AI." It is **who does the cognitive work**. When AI produces the answer, performance rises and learning falls (Turkey, China panel, Lehmann). When AI forces the student to generate, explain and get feedback, under a teacher's or program's structure, learning rises (Nigeria, Harvard, Tutor CoPilot).
- "Better homework, worse exams" is directly relevant to Chinese parents. Homework scores and speed are misleading signals. Parents should judge AI use by closed-book performance, such as dictation (默写), unaided quizzes and timed practice.
- Guardrails alone (GPT Tutor) prevented harm but did not produce gains in Turkey. The positive studies combine guardrails with curriculum alignment, human facilitation, accurate reference solutions and fixed sessions. A Socratic mode on its own is necessary but probably not sufficient.
- The positive meta-analytic g≈0.87 conflicts with the RCTs above. The gap is best explained by many small, short studies that measure assisted performance. Prefer the large pre-registered field RCTs when advising parents.
- Low voluntary engagement (Khanmigo, rural China) means children rarely use a hint-giving tutor well unless an adult or routine makes them.
- Gains concentrate among students with stronger prior knowledge or more advantaged backgrounds (Lehmann; China university RCT), so unstructured AI may widen gaps. The exception is human-AI support for weak tutors (Tutor CoPilot), which helped the least-served most.

### Gaps
- No fetched effect-size magnitude for Google's Sierra Leone RCT. It is company-published.
- Exact Nigeria sample size not retrieved; versions of the paper differ on the ITT estimate (0.21 vs 0.23 SD).
- Kestin quantile-regression effect size (larger than 0.63) not retrieved.
- No RCT found on long-term (>1 year) retention effects of AI tutoring for K-12 children, or on children under about 10 using LLM chatbots for study.
- Effect sizes for the Chinese grade-10 LLM-feedback experiments not obtained. A broader search of Chinese-language journals (e.g., 《电化教育研究》《中国电化教育》) for causal studies was not done in this session.

## 2. What does learning science say about using AI without outsourcing thinking?

### Takeaway
The most robust learning-science principles all depend on **effortful generation by the learner**: retrieval practice, spacing, desirable difficulties, productive struggle or failure, self-explanation, and writing-to-learn. AI helps when it is used to *create* retrieval and feedback opportunities (quizzes, hints, critique of the child's own attempt). It harms when it *removes* the effort (giving answers, writing drafts). The evidence above (Bastani, Lehmann, Kosmyna order effect, OECD) is a direct empirical demonstration of these principles in the AI context.

### Cited Findings
- Productive failure: a meta-analysis of 53 studies (166 comparisons) found problem-solving *before* instruction outperformed instruction-first, Hedges' g=0.36 (95% CI 0.20–0.51). It rose to g=0.37–0.58 when Productive Failure design principles were closely followed. An aggregator reports a bias-corrected g=0.87; verify before citing. — Sinha & Kapur (2021), *Review of Educational Research* — [OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4KnMgZq7); [X-MOL](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1406031499744456704)
- Retrieval practice ("testing effect"): repeated testing produced better delayed retention than repeated study, even though students *felt* re-studying worked better. — Roediger & Karpicke (2006), *Psychological Science*, [DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x) (from prior knowledge; not re-verified this session)
- Of 10 common study techniques, practice testing and distributed practice were rated "high utility." Highlighting and rereading were rated "low utility." — Dunlosky et al. (2013), *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, [DOI 10.1177/1529100612453266](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266) (prior knowledge; not re-verified)
- Writing-to-learn interventions produce small positive effects on academic achievement, larger when they include metacognitive prompts. — Bangert-Drowns, Hurley & Wilkinson (2004), *Review of Educational Research*, [DOI 10.3102/00346543074001029](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543074001029) (prior knowledge; not re-verified)
- Metacognitive illusion with AI: GPT Tutor users overestimated their performance (Bastani), and LLM access inflated perceived learning (Lehmann). — [KQED](https://kqed.org/mindshift/64575/kids-who-use-chatgpt-as-a-study-assistant-do-worse-on-tests); [Lehmann arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09047v2)
- Order effect: doing it yourself first and then using AI preserved more neural engagement than starting with AI [LAB, PREPRINT, small n]. — [IBM Think summary of Kosmyna et al.](https://www.ibm.com/think/news/when-ai-thinks-brain-gets-quieter)
- OECD 2026 names "metacognitive laziness" as the core long-run risk of offloading to chatbots. — [EU Digital Skills summary of OECD DEO 2026](https://digital-skills-jobs.europa.eu/en/latest/news/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026-how-generative-ai-can-support-learning-when-used)
- Explanation-seeking use helped and solution-seeking use hurt (Lehmann et al.). — [Warwick summary](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/cross_fac/eduport/edufund/projects/yang/projects/ai-meets-the-classroom-when-do-large-language-models-harm-learning/)

### Inferences
- Practical rule derived from the evidence: **"Attempt first, AI second, closed-book check last"** (先自己做 → 再问AI → 合上AI再做一遍). The first step preserves productive struggle, the second gives feedback and explanation, and the third is retrieval practice that also exposes the illusion of competence.
- The best AI uses for learning are **AI as quizmaster** (generate questions and grade my recall), **AI as critic** (find errors in *my* solution or essay without fixing them), and **AI as explainer after an attempt**. The worst use is AI as a ghostwriter or answer key.
- Children's feeling that "AI helped me understand" is not reliable evidence. Parents should rely on unaided checks.

### Gaps
- No direct studies found testing specific AI-quizzing (retrieval practice via chatbot) routines with children. The recommendation is an extrapolation from general learning science.
- Classic learning-science citations (Roediger & Karpicke; Dunlosky; Bangert-Drowns) were cited from prior knowledge and not re-fetched in this session. Bjork's "desirable difficulties" also lacks a fetched source here.

## 3. What study/learning modes do major AI products offer (as of 2026), and are there evaluations?

### Takeaway
All three major Western labs now ship a "don't just give the answer" mode: ChatGPT Study Mode (July 2025; folded into ChatGPT for Teens with parent-controllable default-on in August 2026), Gemini Guided Learning (LearnLM-based), and Claude Learning Mode (April 2025 for Claude for Education, later for all users). Parental controls exist (OpenAI parental controls and teen accounts; Google Family Link for under-13 Gemini). **No independent controlled evaluation of these consumer study modes on learning outcomes was found.** The only outcome RCT is Google's own Sierra Leone study, and the closest independent analogue (Bastani's GPT Tutor) shows that a hint-only design prevents harm but does not by itself add learning.

### Cited Findings
- **OpenAI**: ChatGPT for Teens launched 18 Aug 2026. Study Mode uses guiding questions and step-by-step support instead of answers. Parents can switch it on from Parental Controls, after which it is on by default for new chats. A "study hours" setting starts new chats in study mode during chosen hours without blocking access. Other controls: quiet hours and notifications. Parents **cannot read** the teen's conversations. Rolling out to eligible teen accounts on Free and paid plans. — [OpenAI "ChatGPT for Teens"](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-for-teens/); [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/articles/20001421); [Axios, 18 Aug 2026](https://axios.com/2026/08/18/openai-chatgpt-for-teens)
- ChatGPT Study Mode originally launched 29 July 2025, using scaffolding, Socratic questioning and exercises. Reviewers note it can rush ahead without asking about goals or prior knowledge. — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/i-tested-chatgpt-5-study-mode-vs-claude-learning-mode-with-7-prompts-heres-the-winner); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91439393/ai-tutor-chatgpt-openai)
- **Google**: Gemini Guided Learning is described as a "personal AI learning companion" for deeper understanding of complex subjects. — [Google blog, Safer Internet Day 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/safety-security/safer-internet-day-2026-kids-teens/); [Gemini Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16109150). Under-13 Gemini access requires a parent to enable it via Family Link on a supervised account. — [Gemini Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16109150?hl=en); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/kids-under-13-will-soon-get-supervised-access-to-google-gemini-164017036.html). Gemini is also available inside Google Classroom for K-12. — [Axios](https://axios.com/2026/08/18/openai-chatgpt-for-teens)
- LearnLM evaluation: in expert role-play comparisons, pedagogy experts preferred LearnLM over GPT-4o (+31%), Claude 3.5 Sonnet (+11%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (+13%). This measures rated tutoring quality, not student learning. — [LearnLM arXiv 2412.16429](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16429v3). The Sierra Leone RCT (n=1,763) is covered in Section 1. — [Google Cloud LearnLM](https://cloud.google.com/solutions/learnlm?hl=nl)
- **Anthropic**: Claude Learning Mode was introduced April 2025 for Claude for Education users and later extended to all users and developers (Claude Code learning/explanatory styles). It takes a Socratic approach that guides rather than answers. Testers report it asks many questions about goals before helping, and it will switch to direct help (flashcards, practice problems) if asked. — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/anthropic-brings-claudes-learning-mode-to-regular-users-and-devs-170018471.html); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/claudes-new-learning-modes-take-on-chatgpts-study-mode-heres-what-they-do); [Northeastern student guide](https://learning.northeastern.edu/ai-student-guides-using-claude-learning-mode-to-study)
- **Khan Academy Khanmigo**: hint-based tutor integrated into Khan Academy. Independent RCT shows small gains no better than non-AI practice (Section 1). The company ran internal A/B tests Oct 2025–Apr 2026 to improve it; these are not learning-outcome evaluations. — [Khan Academy blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=21314)
- Evaluations of consumer study modes so far are informal journalist or blogger comparisons only. — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/i-tested-chatgpt-5-study-mode-vs-claude-learning-mode-with-7-prompts-heres-the-winner); [Why Try AI](https://www.whytryai.com/p/ai-study-modes)
- Safety evaluations: Common Sense Media rated social AI companions "Unacceptable" for minors (Apr 2025). It rated AI teacher assistants (Gemini in Classroom, Khanmigo Teacher Assistant, MagicSchool, Curipod) "Moderate Risk" (Aug 2025). It found ChatGPT and Sora pose risks to teens despite safety features, and that major chatbots are unsafe for teen mental-health support (Nov 2025). — [Common Sense AI ratings](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/ai-ratings); [AI companions release](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/ai-companions-decoded-common-sense-media-recommends-ai-companion-safety-standards); [Teacher assistants release](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/ai-teacher-assistants-need-better-safety-measures-common-sense-media-report-finds); [ChatGPT/Sora release](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/common-sense-media-report-finds-chatgpt-and-sora-pose-risks-to-teens-despite-safety-features); [Mental health release](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/common-sense-media-finds-major-ai-chatbots-unsafe-for-teen-mental-health-support)
- **China policy context**: The MoE Basic Education Teaching Guidance Committee issued 《中小学生成式人工智能使用指南(2025年版)》 in May 2025. It sets differentiated use by school stage. Primary pupils are prohibited from using open-ended content generation on their own, and teachers may use it in class (reported as use with teacher or parent help). Lower secondary may explore logical analysis of generated content, and upper secondary may do inquiry learning on technical principles. Students are prohibited from directly copying AI-generated content as homework or exam answers. Teachers must not use GenAI as a substitute teaching subject or use AI output directly to evaluate students. Reported via news outlets, not checked against the original text. — [中国新闻网](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2025/05-12/10414191.shtml); [中国教育在线 (EOL) text](https://www.eol.cn/zhengce/wenjian/202505/t20250512_2667831.shtml); [澎湃新闻](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_30802716); [新华网](https://www.news.cn/talking/20250522/d6bed8f9186c4442b64c2b7861aafff5/c.html)

### Inferences
- For Chinese parents, Western products may be unavailable or restricted in mainland China. The same design principle (hint, don't tell) can be imposed on any domestic model (豆包, DeepSeek, Kimi, 通义, or learning-device AIs from 作业帮/学而思/科大讯飞) with a standing system prompt or custom instructions. The evidence is about design, not brand.
- Turning on study mode is the minimum bar. It addresses the Bastani "GPT Base" harm, but the positive results also needed structure (fixed sessions, teacher or parent check-ins, accurate solutions).
- Because parents cannot read teen chats on ChatGPT (privacy by design), oversight shifts to outcome checks (closed-book) and routines, not surveillance.

### Gaps
- Could not verify Gemini Guided Learning's exact launch date (widely reported as Aug 2025) or whether it is on by default for teens.
- No information gathered on Chinese domestic products' study modes or parental controls (e.g., 豆包 youth mode, 学习机 AI tutors) or on any evaluations of them. This is a significant gap for the target audience.
- No independent RCT of ChatGPT Study Mode, Claude Learning Mode or Gemini Guided Learning found.

## 4. How reliable are AI-detection tools, and what does that imply for homework and academic integrity?

### Takeaway
AI-text detectors are unreliable and biased against non-native writers. **More than half of non-native English (TOEFL) essays were falsely flagged** in a Stanford test. Detectors are also easily evaded by paraphrasing, and OpenAI withdrew its own detector. Detection cannot be the basis for parenting or school integrity. Process-based evidence (drafts, oral explanation, in-class or closed-book work) is the defensible alternative.

### Cited Findings
- Liang, Zou et al. (Stanford; *Patterns* 2023, arXiv 2304.02819): 7 detectors on 91 TOEFL essays and 88 U.S. 8th-grade essays. Near-perfect on native essays, but an **average false-positive rate of 61.22% on TOEFL essays**. All 7 flagged 18/91 (19.8%), and at least one flagged 89/91 (97.8%). The mechanism is low perplexity (simpler vocabulary). Prompting for more diverse language removed the bias and also let GPT text evade detection. Zou: avoid relying on detectors in education, especially with many non-native speakers. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02819v3); [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-detectors-biased-against-non-native-english-writers)
- Weber-Wulff et al. (2023), *International Journal for Educational Integrity* 19:26: tested 14 tools, including Turnitin and PlagiarismCheck. Concluded the tools are "neither accurate nor reliable" and biased toward classifying text as human-written. Obfuscation and machine translation further reduced accuracy. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s40979-023-00146-z); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15666v2)
- OpenAI shut down its AI Text Classifier in July 2023 for "low rate of accuracy." At launch it correctly identified only 26% of AI-written text (missed 74%). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/25/openai-scuttles-ai-written-text-detector-over-low-rate-of-accuracy/?guccounter=1)
- 72% of lower-secondary teachers (TALIS 2024) believe AI can harm academic integrity. — [EU summary of OECD DEO 2026](https://digital-skills-jobs.europa.eu/en/latest/news/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026-how-generative-ai-can-support-learning-when-used)
- China's 2025 guideline prohibits copying AI output as homework or exam answers and bars teachers from using AI output directly to evaluate students. — [中国新闻网](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2025/05-12/10414191.shtml)

### Inferences
- For Chinese families: children writing in English as a second language are exactly the group most likely to be falsely accused by English-language detectors. Parents should know this if a school abroad or an international school flags a child.
- Homework is losing its value as a measure of learning (China panel: homework +18% while exams −20%). Parents and schools should shift weight to work done in front of them: dictation, oral explanation ("讲给我听"), timed unaided practice, and handwritten drafts with visible revision history.
- An integrity norm is more workable than detection: "AI may help you check and explain, but every submitted sentence and step must be something you can reproduce and explain without it."

### Gaps
- No recent (2025–2026) independent accuracy benchmark of current commercial detectors (Turnitin AI, GPTZero, Chinese tools such as CNKI's AIGC detection used for theses) was retrieved. No data found on Chinese-language AIGC detector false-positive rates, though Chinese universities widely use them for theses since 2024–2025.
- Watermarking (e.g., Google SynthID text) was not researched.

## 5. Concrete, evidence-based practices for parents (prompts, routines, rules) by age

### Takeaway
No RCT tests parental AI rules directly, so age-specific guidance must be **inferred** from the causal studies, learning science, China's MoE stage-based guideline, and product age rules. A defensible framework: **under about 10, no independent open-ended chatbot use for schoolwork (adult-co-used only); ages 10–14, supervised hint-only tutoring in fixed sessions after an own attempt; 15+, broader independent use under an "attempt, then AI, then closed-book check" routine, with AI as quizmaster and critic, never ghostwriter.** Measure success by unaided performance, not homework quality.

### Cited Findings (evidence anchors for the practices)
- Hint-only design removes the harm of answer-giving AI (GPT Tutor ≈ control vs Base −17%). — [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education)
- Gains appear with structure: fixed sessions, teacher-guided prompts, curriculum alignment (Nigeria about 13 hrs/6 wks → +0.23 SD; Harvard tutor with step-by-step scaffolding and accurate solutions). — [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/education/From-chalkboards-to-chatbots-Transforming-learning-in-Nigeria); [PMC Kestin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/)
- Children rarely use a tutor's help voluntarily: messaging on about a third of practice days and 17% of error sessions. — [NBER Khanmigo](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620)
- Explanations help, solutions hurt; weaker students are hurt more. — [Lehmann et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09047v2)
- Own work first, then AI, preserved engagement [LAB, PREPRINT]. — [IBM summary of Kosmyna](https://www.ibm.com/think/news/when-ai-thinks-brain-gets-quieter)
- Problem solving before instruction improves learning (g=0.36). — [Sinha & Kapur](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4KnMgZq7)
- China MoE stage rules: primary, no independent open-ended generation; secondary, gradually more analytical use; no copying AI output into homework. — [中国新闻网](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2025/05-12/10414191.shtml)
- Product age gates: Gemini for under-13s only via a parent-enabled Family Link supervised account; ChatGPT teen accounts with parental controls, study-mode default and quiet hours. — [Gemini Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16109150?hl=en); [OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/articles/20001421)
- AI companions rated unacceptable for minors; chatbots unsafe for teen mental-health support, so keep study AI separate from emotional-support use. — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/ai-ratings)
- Overconfidence and illusion of learning with AI. — [KQED on Bastani](https://kqed.org/mindshift/64575/kids-who-use-chatgpt-as-a-study-assistant-do-worse-on-tests)

### Inferences (practical recommendations derived from the above; not themselves tested)

**By age (aligned with the MoE stage guideline)**
- **Ages 6–9 (lower primary):** No solo chatbot for homework. Parent-co-use only, for curiosity questions ("let's ask together, then check in a book"). Core skills (characters, arithmetic facts, reading) stay AI-free. AI's role, if any, is making quiz cards that the parent administers.
- **Ages 10–12 (upper primary):** Short (15–20 min), scheduled, parent-present sessions on a hint-only setup. "Three-attempt rule": the child must show a written attempt before asking. The parent ends with one closed-book question ("now do a similar one without AI").
- **Ages 13–15 (junior secondary/中考 years):** Teen account with study mode on by default. Study hours inside homework time, quiet hours at night. AI allowed for explanation after an attempt, error analysis (错题本: "why was my step 3 wrong?"), and generating extra practice. AI is never allowed for writing essays (作文) or producing answers to assigned problems. A weekly unaided self-test is the real KPI.
- **Ages 16–18 (senior secondary/高考):** More autonomy, with the same "attempt, then AI, then closed-book" routine. Add explicit AI literacy: verify facts, ask for sources, spot hallucinations. For writing, the teen drafts first and AI critiques; AI does not rewrite. Discuss integrity norms openly.

**Prompt templates (to paste as standing instructions in any model, including domestic ones)**
- Tutor guardrail: "你是我的家教。不要直接告诉我答案或完整解法。每次只给一个提示,先问我已经试了什么。我答对后再让我解释为什么。" (You are my tutor. Never give the answer or the full solution. Give one hint at a time and first ask what I've tried. When I get it right, make me explain why.)
- Quizmaster (retrieval practice): "根据这一章内容出5道题,一次一道,等我回答后再批改并告诉我错在哪,最后总结我需要复习的点。" (Write 5 questions on this chapter, one at a time. Mark each after I answer and say where I went wrong. At the end, list what I need to review.)
- Critic (writing-to-learn): "这是我写的作文。指出3个最大的问题,但不要帮我改写任何句子。" (Here is my essay. Point out the 3 biggest problems, but don't rewrite any sentences.)
- Error analysis: "这是我的解题过程,请找出第一个出错的步骤,只告诉我哪一步、为什么,不要给出正确答案。" (Here is my working. Find the first wrong step and tell me only which step and why. Don't give the correct answer.)
- Explain-back: "我来给你讲这个概念,你扮演一个不懂的同学,追问我讲不清楚的地方。" (I'll explain this concept to you. Play a classmate who doesn't understand, and press me wherever my explanation is unclear.)

**Household rules**
1. Attempt first (先做再问). 2. AI gives hints, not answers. 3. Close AI and redo one (合上再做一遍). 4. Judge progress by unaided tests, not homework. 5. No AI for emotional companionship, at least until older teens, and talk about it. 6. Devices out of the bedroom at night (use quiet hours). 7. Never paste AI text into submitted work. Every submitted line must be explainable aloud.

### Gaps
- No RCT or quasi-experiment found on parent-implemented AI rules or home routines. All age cutoffs above are inferences, anchored to the MoE guideline and product age policies rather than direct causal evidence.
- No evidence found on optimal session length or frequency for children's AI tutoring beyond the program designs studied (Nigeria: about 13 hours over 6 weeks, sourced above. Bastani's session structure, recalled as four 90-min sessions, is unverified here).
- No Chinese-language parent-facing evaluation studies (e.g., 学习机 AI tutoring at home) found in this session.
