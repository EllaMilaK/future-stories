# Parental Anxiety, Intensive Parenting (鸡娃/内卷), and Evidence-Based Family Practices for Technology and AI

Research notes compiled 2026-11-24. Scope: roughly 22 searches and fetches. Most of the evidence comes from search-result abstracts and summaries. Full texts were not read unless noted, so exact effect sizes should be checked against the primary papers before the book quotes them. The labels [CN], [West] and [Cross-cultural] mark where each sample came from.

---

## Q1. Intensive parenting, academic pressure and child outcomes in China and East Asia

### Takeaway
Across Chinese and Western samples, controlling, punitive "tiger"-style parenting has a weak and inconsistent link to grades but a consistent link to worse emotional outcomes, especially anxiety and depression. Supportive parenting, including warm Chinese parents who are highly involved, does best. Chinese adolescent depressive *symptom* screening rates cluster around 22–29%. That is very different from diagnosed depression, which a national health official put at about 2%. The book should not merge the two figures.

### Cited Findings
**Tiger parenting and control**
- [Chinese American] Kim et al. followed 444 Chinese American families over 8 years. They identified four profiles: supportive, tiger, easygoing and harsh. Supportive parenting was the most common and had the best developmental outcomes, and tiger parenting was not the typical profile. — [APA Monitor 2013](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2013/09/tiger-parenting); [PMC3641860](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3641860); [UC News](https://universityofcalifornia.edu/news/hold-tiger-mom)
- [CN, nationally representative] A 2014 China Family Panel Studies analysis linked maternal control over education decisions to better cognitive and academic scores but weaker non-cognitive skill development. The design is correlational. — [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/tiger-mom-parenting-boosts-teens-cognitive-skills-but-undermines-emotional-development-study-suggests/)
- [CN, longitudinal] Helicopter and tiger parenting worsened the trajectories of children's anxiety and depression, and children's inhibitory control buffered the risk. — [Child Psychiatry & Human Development, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10578-024-01685-3)
- [US and CN, 806 seventh graders, 6-month longitudinal; Wang, Pomerantz and colleagues] Psychological control harmed children's emotional and academic functioning in both countries, with similar effect strengths. — [Univ. of Illinois news](https://news.illinois.edu/study-shows-parenting-styles-have-similar-effects-in-china-and-u-s/)
- Conflicting evidence: a 2024 review notes that some Chinese studies found psychological control did *not* significantly predict lower academic functioning, and some found only nonsignificant associations. The academic harm is less consistent than the emotional harm. — [Behavioral Sciences 2024, PMC10968045](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10968045)
- [US and CN, 825 adolescents] Parents' heightened *involvement*, as distinct from control, predicted better engagement and achievement in both the US and China. — [Cheung & Pomerantz 2011, PMC3089668](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3089668)
- Ng, Pomerantz & Deng (2012) argue that Chinese parents' self-worth is more contingent on their children's performance, which helps explain why Chinese parents use more control. This is a useful mechanism for a book about parental anxiety. — [PMC3714336](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3714336)
- [CN] Longitudinal data on young adolescents: higher perceived psychological control predicted membership in higher-depression trajectories, and autonomy support predicted lower risk. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2021, Fudan](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.681940/full)
- [CN, 310 adolescents] Gaps between parents' and adolescents' perceptions of psychological control predicted higher adolescent depression. — [PMC12137482](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12137482/)
- Cultural-normativity argument: controlling practices may be more normative in China and therefore read by children as more acceptable (Cheung & Pomerantz 2011, cited in) — [PMC8267414](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8267414/). Later cross-cultural meta-analytic work still finds psychological control harmful in collectivist samples (China, South Korea, Ghana) as well as individualist ones. — [PMC12729761](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12729761/)

**Involution (内卷)**
- [CN, 535 college students] Involution was not uniformly linked to anxiety. Passive involution and achievement-motivated involution correlated with anxiety in opposite directions. — [IJERPH 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/19/16/9826)
- [CN, 70 high schoolers, small conference paper] Involution correlated positively with anxiety. The paper sorted involution into enjoyment, utilitarian and coercion types. This is weak evidence. — [Atlantis Press 2024](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/126009441.pdf)
- Qualitative interviews in Zhejiang with students, parents and teachers on gaokao and job pressure ("involuted generation"). — [Journal for Undergraduate Ethnography 2024](https://ojs.library.dal.ca/JUE/article/view/12047)

**Prevalence of depression in Chinese youth**
- 《中国国民心理健康发展报告（2019–2020）》 (CAS Institute of Psychology): the adolescent depression-*symptom* detection rate was 24.6%, and 7.4% for severe symptoms. This measures symptoms in the past week, not a clinical diagnosis. — [中新网 2021](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2021/10-18/9588781.shtml); [澎湃](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_15092236)
- An expert at a National Health Commission press conference in late 2024 said epidemiological surveys put the prevalence of adolescent depressive *disorder* at about 2%. — [第一财经](https://www.yicai.com/news/102417254.html)
- 《心理健康蓝皮书（2023–2024）》 was released in April 2025 with more than 170,000 samples from 79 institutions. Its youth report emphasizes family and school support, and left-behind adolescents showed higher depression and anxiety risk. No headline youth rate was found. — [界面](https://www.jiemian.com/article/7343618.html); [澎湃](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_30698909)
- Meta-analyses: pooled prevalence of depressive symptoms was 22.2% for 1988–2018 [PMC6792515](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6792515); 28.6% during COVID (13 studies, about 41,700 participants) [PMC9023859](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9023859/); 26.17% across 439 studies with about 1.5 million participants, I² = 100% and *no clear temporal trend* [PMC11577650](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11577650). The 2019 and 2024 reviews disagree about whether the rate is rising.

**Double Reduction policy (双减, 2021)**
- [CN, 395 primary pupils, 3 schools in Yuncheng] Homework and screen time fell and sleep rose after the policy. Anxiety correlated positively with homework and screen time and negatively with exercise and sleep. — [PMC11667910](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11667910)
- A Communist Youth League (共青团) and China Youth Daily survey of 511,043 parents found 87.0% felt anxious and 72.7% said their education anxiety had eased. The survey was self-administered and run by a party-affiliated body. — [新华网 2022](https://www.news.cn/politics/2022-01/24/c_1128293375.htm)
- A Frontiers 2025 study of 2,932 parents links family economic background to education anxiety under 双减 and notes ongoing controversy. — [Frontiers in Psychiatry 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1525651/full)

### Inferences
- The book's best evidence-based frame: the problem is not high expectations or involvement, which predict good outcomes in China too. The problem is *psychological control*, meaning guilt, love withdrawal and pressure tied to the parent's own self-worth.
- Present the "24.6%" figure carefully. It is a screening rate for symptoms, not a diagnosis rate. Citing it as "a quarter of kids have depression" would mislead readers.

### Gaps
- I found no meta-analysis specifically of "tiger parenting" outcomes. Evidence comes from individual studies and from psychological-control meta-analyses.
- Peer-reviewed adolescent research on 内卷 is thin and mostly uses university students or small samples.
- No 2025–2026 national figure for youth depressive-symptom rates was found.

## Q2. How parental anxiety transmits to children, and which interventions help

### Takeaway
Children of anxious parents are at about 1.8 times the risk of anxiety disorders. Parent-focused treatment that reduces "accommodation" (SPACE) matched child CBT in an RCT. Autonomy support relates robustly to child wellbeing, at roughly r = 0.30 meta-analytically and across cultures.

### Cited Findings
- Lawrence, Murayama & Creswell (2019, JAACAP; 25 studies, 7,285 offspring): offspring of parents with anxiety disorders had RR 1.76 (95% CI 1.58–1.96) for anxiety disorders and RR 1.31 (1.13–1.52) for depressive disorders. Parental panic disorder and GAD carried particular risk, and transmission of the *same* specific disorder had limited support. — [PubMed 30577938](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30577938/)
- Earlier meta-analysis (2009; 16 papers, 1,892 offspring): odds ratios were 3.91 for anxiety and 2.67 for depression versus non-psychiatric controls. These are odds ratios and not comparable with the 2019 risk ratios. — [PubMed 19709850](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19709850/)
- A 2024 meta-analysis also links *paternal* anxiety to offspring emotional and behavioral outcomes. — [PubMed 38697345](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38697345/)
- [West] SPACE (Supportive Parenting for Anxious Childhood Emotions) RCT, Lebowitz et al. 2020, JAACAP: 124 anxious children aged 7–14 were randomized to 12 sessions of parent-only SPACE or 12 sessions of child CBT. SPACE was *noninferior* on primary and secondary outcomes rated by clinicians, parents and children. It works by reducing parental accommodation, such as reassurance or avoiding feared situations for the child. — [PMC6732048](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6732048); [NIH Research Matters](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/supportive-parenting-can-reduce-childs-anxiety); [Yale News](https://news.yale.edu/2019/03/12/new-way-combat-childhood-anxiety-treat-parents)
- Autonomy support (self-determination theory): Vasquez et al. (2016; 36 studies) linked parent autonomy support to academic achievement and psychosocial functioning, possibly moderated by grade level. A later review cites r ≈ .38 for psychological health; that figure is second-hand. — [Educational Psychology Review](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10648-015-9329-z); [PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/2016_VasquezPatallFongCorriganPine_EPR.pdf)
- Bradshaw et al. (American Psychologist, 2025; k = 238): autonomy support related to child wellbeing at r ≈ 0.30. Psychological control and autonomy support each contributed uniquely, the effects held across cultures, and the meta-analysis tested cross-cultural generalizability. — [Preprint](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/2024_BradshawDuinevelEtAl_ParentingMeta_PrePrint.pdf); [PubMed 39052356](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39052356/)

### Inferences
- SPACE's core message fits AI-anxious parents: a parent's job is to say "I believe you can cope" and to stop accommodating the child's anxiety, *and* to manage their own. This applies to AI-driven educational fear as well. Note that SPACE treats child anxiety; it is not a treatment for parental anxiety itself.
- The Ng/Pomerantz finding that parents' self-worth is contingent on children's performance offers a mechanism for transmission in Chinese families.

### Gaps
- SPACE effect sizes, any Chinese-language adaptation trials and replications were not found.
- No Chinese-sample RCTs of autonomy-support parent training were located.

## Q3. Parental mediation, family media plans and parents' own device use

### Takeaway
Meta-analytic effects of any media-mediation style are small (|r| ≈ .01–.11) and mostly correlational. Active mediation, meaning talking with children about media, has the best support for risk outcomes such as aggression and substance use. Restrictive rules help little, except perhaps for older teens. Parents' own phone use ("technoference" or phubbing) correlates moderately with child problems, including in Chinese samples (r ≈ .21–.30).

### Cited Findings
- Collier et al. (2016, Developmental Psychology; 57 studies): restrictive mediation r = −.06, co-viewing r = .09, active mediation not significant overall. Active mediation related to lower aggression (r = −.08), sexual behavior (r = −.06) and substance use (r = −.11). The author's thesis version gave somewhat different estimates. — [BYU ScholarsArchive](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/4021); [thesis](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/etd/5831/)
- 2022 meta-analysis on problematic internet use: active mediation (r = −0.02) and restrictive mediation (r = 0.01) were not significant overall, but restrictive mediation was significant for older adolescents. — via [search summary; Addictive Behaviors 2022] (primary URL not retrieved; see Gaps)
- 2023 meta-analysis (41 studies, Canadian Journal of Behavioural Science): active mediation and co-use were *positively* correlated with problematic media use, likely reverse causation where parents step in after problems appear. Restrictive mediation was not significant. — (primary URL not retrieved; see Gaps)
- Parents' negative attitudes toward media predict restrictive mediation (r = .161), and parental involvement predicts active mediation (r = .156). — [J Child Fam Stud 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10826-022-02459-y)
- AAP "5 Cs" framework: Child, Content, Calm, Crowding Out and Communication. AAP recommends asking whether media "crowds out" what the family values rather than counting hours, and points families to the AAP Family Media Plan. — [AAP 5Cs](https://www.aap.org/5Cs); [HealthyChildren](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/Kids-and-Screen-Time-How-to-Use-the-5-Cs-of-Media-Guidance.aspx)
- A 2026 AAP news release says a new policy statement and technical report ("creating a child-friendly digital world") replace earlier AAP media guidance. I could not fetch it because aap.org is blocked in this environment. — [AAP 2026 release](https://www.aap.org/en/news-room/news-releases/aap/2026/american-academy-of-pediatrics-calls-for-creating-a-child-friendly-digital-world/)
- Parents' own device use:
  - [West, 170 US families, child age about 3] Parents' problematic tech use predicted technoference, and mothers' technoference predicted child externalizing and internalizing behavior. — [McDaniel & Radesky 2018, PubMed 28493400](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28493400/); [Pediatric Research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41390-018-0052-6)
  - Meta-analysis: parental technoference correlated with children's problematic media use (r = 0.296, 95% CI 0.259–0.331). The link was stronger when both parents did it and in cross-sectional studies than longitudinal ones. — [JMIR 2025](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e57636)
  - [CN] Meta-analysis of Chinese parental-phubbing studies: internalizing problems r = 0.270 and externalizing problems r = 0.210. — [PMC10591670](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10591670)
  - Caveat: a multiverse analysis (Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry, 2020) found that direct smartphone–parenting associations were weak and mixed. — [PMC7496662](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/7496662)

### Inferences
- For the book, a strict "screen time limit" message is not evidence-based. The stronger levers are conversation (active mediation), modeling, and protecting sleep, exercise and reading from displacement. The parent-phubbing data from Chinese samples is the most actionable finding for adult behavior.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the content of AAP's 2026 policy, or whether the Family Media Plan was revised. aap.org must be allowed in network settings to check.
- No RCT evidence was found that family media plans change child outcomes.
- Primary URLs for the 2022 and 2023 problematic-use meta-analyses were not retrieved.

## Q4. Building durable traits: motivation, mindset, grit, reading, play, chores, sports, boredom, SEL

### Takeaway
The best-supported levers are autonomy support (for intrinsic motivation), school-based SEL, physical activity (moderate effect on depressive symptoms) and reading for pleasure (strong association). Growth-mindset interventions have tiny average effects (d ≈ 0.02–0.14) and sit at the centre of a live replication debate. Grit is largely conscientiousness repackaged and predicts academic success at about r = .18. Evidence on chores and boredom is weak and correlational.

### Cited Findings
- Growth mindset:
  - Sisk et al. 2018 (Psychological Science): two meta-analyses, mindset and achievement (k = 273) and interventions (k = 43), both found weak effects overall. — [PDF](https://englelab.gatech.edu/articles/2018/Sisk,%20Burgoyne%20et%20al.%20(2018)%20-%20Mindset%20and%20Academic%20Achievement.pdf)
  - Macnamara & Burgoyne 2023 (Psychological Bulletin) attribute apparent effects to poor design, reporting flaws and bias; the six highest-quality studies gave d = 0.02. Burnette et al. 2023 (Psychological Bulletin) find effects for targeted, high-fidelity focal groups (about d = 0.14) but about d = 0.04 otherwise. — [PubMed 36326645](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36326645/); [Commentary PMC10495100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10495100/); [Purdue PDF](https://hhs.purdue.edu/skill-learning-and-performance-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2024/08/2023-90931-004.pdf)
- Grit (Credé, Tynan & Harms 2017, JPSP; 88 studies, about 67,000 people): grit correlates very highly with conscientiousness (about .84 at the construct level, as reported). Its correlation with academic performance is r ≈ .18. The perseverance facet adds some predictive value; the consistency-of-interest facet does not. — [PubMed 27845531](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27845531/); [Iowa State](https://www.news.iastate.edu/news/2016/05/18/grit-analysis); [Education Next](https://www.educationnext.org/much-ado-about-grit-interview-with-a-leading-psych-researcher/)
- SEL (Cipriano et al. 2023, Child Development; 424 studies, 53 countries, 575,361 students, 2008–2020): universal school SEL improved skills, attitudes, behaviors, school climate, peer relationships and academic achievement. The largest effect was on perceived school safety and inclusion, and skill gains persisted six months or more. — [Child Development](https://academic.oup.com/chidev/article-abstract/94/5/1181/8255238); [Yale](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/new-research-published-in-child-development-confirms-social-and-emotional-learning-significantly-improves-student-academic-performance-well-being-and-perceptions-of-school-safety/)
- Reading for pleasure (Sun et al. 2023, Psychological Medicine; ABCD, 10,243 US children): starting early (3–10 years of pleasure reading) was associated with better adolescent cognition, fewer stress and depression symptoms, better attention, larger brain volumes, less screen time and longer sleep. The design is observational, and the comparison group merged non-readers with late starters. It is a US sample, though the research team included Chinese researchers (Fudan). — [Cambridge](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/reading-for-pleasure-early-in-childhood-linked-to-better-cognitive-performance-and-mental-wellbeing)
- Physical activity:
  - 2023 JAMA Pediatrics meta-analysis (21 RCTs, 2,441 participants): g = −0.29 for depressive symptoms; the effect was not maintained at follow-up. — [PubMed 36595284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36595284/)
  - Umbrella meta-meta-analysis (180 RCTs, 34,490 participants): SMD = −0.45 with high heterogeneity. — [PubMed 40239946](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40239946/)
- Chores (Tepper et al. 2022, Australian Occupational Therapy Journal; 207 children aged 5–13): self-care and family-care chores were associated with better executive function. The study is cross-sectional and parent-reported, so causation is unknown and confounding by socioeconomic status is possible. — [PubMed 35640882](https://www.pubmed.gov/35640882/); [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/doing-more-chores-linked-better-executive-functioning-kids-direction-effect-unclear)
- Intrinsic motivation through autonomy support: see the Vasquez 2016 and Bradshaw 2025 findings under Q2.

### Inferences
- Book guidance: skip "praise effort and mindset" formulas as the main tool. Prioritize autonomy-supportive daily interactions, reading for fun, daily movement and sleep. Present chores as low-cost and plausible rather than proven.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain Cipriano 2023's pooled effect sizes or Durlak 2011's figures from the search results. Get them from the full text (DOI 10.1111/cdev.13968).
- No meta-analytic evidence was found on unstructured play or boredom and creativity in this pass.
- Mol & Bus (2011) print-exposure meta-analysis was not retrieved.
- Almost all trait evidence comes from Western samples; Chinese replications were not searched.

## Q5. Talking with children about AI: frameworks and resources

### Takeaway
Authoritative family-facing resources now exist. UNICEF Guidance on AI and Children 3.0 (December 2025) sets 10 requirements. Common Sense Media and MIT's Day of AI publish free family toolkits. China's Ministry of Education issued 2025 guidelines that bar primary pupils from using open-ended generative AI alone. Teens are already heavy users: 72% of US teens have used AI companions.

### Cited Findings
- UNICEF Guidance on AI and Children version 3.0, December 2025 (Innocenti): 10 requirements grounded in the Convention on the Rights of the Child, covering regulation, safety, data and privacy, non-discrimination, transparency and accountability, and children's participation, among others. It was updated because of generative AI and was informed by a 12-country study with children and caregivers. The 2020 draft had 9 requirements. — [UNICEF Innocenti](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children); [UNRIC](https://unric.org/en/unicef-guidance-on-ai-and-children/); [UNICEF China](https://www.unicef.cn/en/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children)
- Common Sense Media, "Talk, Trust, and Trade-Offs" (July 2025; 1,060 US teens aged 13–17):
  - 72% have used AI companions and 52% are regular users.
  - 50% don't trust their advice, and younger teens trust it more.
  - About a third felt uncomfortable with something an AI companion said.
  - About a quarter shared personal information such as name or location.
  - [Common Sense press release](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/nearly-3-in-4-teens-have-used-ai-companions-new-national-survey-finds); [report](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/talk-trust-and-trade-offs-how-and-why-teens-use-ai-companions)
- About 75% of teens use AI companion chatbots but only about a third of parents know, as cited in a November 2025 Day of AI and Common Sense release. — [Day of AI](https://dayofai.org/news/day-of-ai-and-common-sense-media-launch-essential-toolkit-for-parents-and-families-talking-to-kids-about-ai-privacy-fairness-and-responsibility)
- Day of AI (MIT RAISE) and Common Sense family toolkits:
  - "An AI Literacy Toolkit: Empowering Families to Explore AI Together" (spring 2025) has videos, conversation cards and activities.
  - "Talking to Kids About AI" (November 2025) covers privacy, fairness and responsibility.
  - "Using AI Wisely for School Success" (February 2026).
  - [Day of AI Families](https://dayofai.org/families); [Common Sense Families AI Literacy Toolkit](https://www.commonsense.org/education/families-ai-literacy-toolkit); [Feb 2026 release](https://dayofai.org/news/day-of-ai-and-common-sense-media-launch-using-ai-wisely-for-school-success-toolkit); [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/parents-need-ai-literacy-lessons-too-a-new-toolkit-aims-to-help/2025/11); [MIT RAISE impact report](https://raise.mit.edu/stories/2025-day-of-ai-impact-report/)
- China, Ministry of Education Basic Education Teaching Guidance Committee (May 2025): 《中小学人工智能通识教育指南（2025年版）》 and 《中小学生成式人工智能使用指南（2025年版）》.
  - Primary pupils should not use open-ended generative functions alone; they should use them with teacher or parent help.
  - Middle school may explore the logic of generated content; high school may do inquiry learning based on technical principles.
  - Students must not copy AI output as homework or exam answers.
  - Teachers must not use AI as a substitute teaching subject or to evaluate students directly.
  - Sensitive data must not be entered into AI tools.
  - The literacy curriculum spirals: interest and basic awareness in primary school, then principles and application, then systems thinking and innovation.
  - [中新网](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2025/05-12/10414191.shtml); [中国教育在线 (full text)](https://www.eol.cn/zhengce/wenjian/202505/t20250512_2667831.shtml); [澎湃](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_30802716)

### Inferences
- The MOE's "use with parent help" rule for primary pupils is the AI version of active mediation and co-use. The book can connect this policy directly to the Q3 evidence.
- Chinese parents are likely to underestimate their children's AI companion use, as US parents do. I found no Chinese survey to confirm this.

### Gaps
- No outcome evaluations of any family AI-literacy toolkit were found; reach claims are self-reported by the organizations.
- I did not find Chinese-sample survey data on children's or teens' AI or chatbot use, nor on parents' AI anxiety.
- The full list of UNICEF 3.0's ten requirements was not retrieved verbatim.
- Peer-reviewed research on children and AI companions (beyond surveys) was not searched.
